\section{Implementation}
\label{sec:imple}

\subsection{Simulation Environment}

% Where are the controllers tested?
The system is simulated in Gazebo~\cite{koenig2004gazebo}, with the hydrodynamics and the waves of the Virtual RobotX environment~\cite{bingham2019vrx}, in which the WAM-V heaves, rolls and pitches on the waves. The x500 is flown by PX4 in software-in-the-loop, so that the NMPC drives it through the same autopilot as on the real vehicle. The tether is simulated with MoorDyn~\cite{hall2015moordyn} as a chain of lumped masses with its own weight, elasticity and drag, so that it pulls on both vehicles through its own dynamics rather than through the model of the controllers. A virtual winch on the deck pays it out or reels it in at up to $2\,\text{m/s}$, and holds the UAV against the deck during the takeoff and the landing. Wind acts on the UAV as a drag force with gusts.

\subsection{Model Validation}

% Are the models accurate enough to predict with?
The models of Section~\ref{sec:model} were compared with the simulator as the controllers use them, over predictions as long as each horizon, started from the state of the simulator and driven by the recorded commands, as listed in Table~\ref{tab:validation}. Over its horizon, the model of the WAM-V predicts its position within a few centimetres, and that of the x500 within about one percent of the distance it travels, an accuracy that relies on the delays of the attitude response. The model of the tether reduces the error of the force by about three quarters with respect to a taut tether or a hanging load, and follows the tension computed by the simulator from the dynamics of the tether using only the positions of the two vehicles, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:tether_fit}.

\subsection{Controller Implementation}

% How are the controllers set up?
The horizons follow the dynamics of each vehicle. The USV NMPC runs at $10\,\text{Hz}$ over $2\,\text{s}$, long enough to see the response of a slow vessel to its thrusters, and the UAV NMPC at $50\,\text{Hz}$ over $1\,\text{s}$. The attitude of the UAV starts to respond to a command only $0.12\,\text{s}$ after it is sent, longer than the response itself takes, which is why the delays are part of the prediction.

% How do the two controllers use each other's prediction?
Each controller resamples the latest predicted trajectory of the other at the nodes of its own horizon, since the two run at different rates. The horizon of the UAV covers only the first second of that of the USV, so the USV enforces the tether constraint only within that window, which slides forward as the problem is solved again at every instant. The USV solver iterates to convergence within its sampling interval. The UAV solver could not always do so within $20\,\text{ms}$, so it performs a single iteration per instant, the real-time iteration~\cite{diehl2005rti}, whose commands stay close to the converged ones. If a solver fails, it is re-initialised at the current state and solved once more.

\subsection{Manoeuvres and Missions}

% Who decides where each vehicle goes?
The controllers follow references, but do not decide where each vehicle should go. This is the role of the manoeuvres, which give the reference of one vehicle over the horizon of its controller at every instant. The UAV can take off, fly in formation with the USV, orbit it or a point in the world, chase a target and land, while the USV can hold its position, follow a route, pursue a target, or support the UAV from the water near a structure, so that the structure stays within the reach of the tether. Since the controllers keep the vehicles within their limits, the references can remain simple, often a single point. Figure~\ref{fig:catalogue} shows the manoeuvres of the UAV as flown in the missions, the takeoff and the landing through the height of the UAV above the winch, and the others from above.

% Why are the takeoff and the landing special?
The takeoff and the landing are the manoeuvres in which the UAV is closest to the deck and moves with it. The takeoff starts with a vertical climb while the winch holds the UAV against the deck, so that it does not drift over its edge at low height. In the landing, the UAV first waits above the pad until it is centred and moving with it, then descends with the winch pulling it, and ends with a slow approach towards a point below the deck, so that it touches down even if the deck falls away.

% How are the manoeuvres combined into missions?
A mission is a file with a sequence of manoeuvres for each vehicle, in which each manoeuvre ends with an event, such as the detection of a target, or with the end of a manoeuvre of the other vehicle. Three missions were tested: a scout, in which the UAV flies ahead of the bow while the USV follows a route, an inspection, in which the UAV circles a tower while the USV supports it, and a patrol, in which the UAV orbits the USV until it detects a target, which it then chases while the USV pursues it.
