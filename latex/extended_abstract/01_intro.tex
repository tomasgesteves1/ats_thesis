\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Why join a USV and a UAV with a tether?
\IEEEPARstart{S}{aving} lives at sea, protecting marine ecosystems and securing maritime infrastructure all rely on a continuous and local awareness of what happens at sea~\cite{europeanmaritimesafetyagencyEMSAOutlook2025_v302025}. Unmanned surface vessels (USVs) keep a presence at sea for long periods at a low cost, but they only see as far as the horizon, while unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) sense from above, but have a short endurance and an unreliable long-range link~\cite{zeng2016uav,kishk2020tethered}. A tether between the two supplies the UAV with power from the energy storage of the USV and with a reliable communication link, which removes both limits at once~\cite{kishk2020tethered}, and such systems are already offered by industry~\cite{exail_elistair_tethered,acua_ocean_teledyne_mod}.

% What makes the tethered pair hard to control?
The tether, however, turns the two vehicles into a single system. The distance between them must stay within the reach of the tether, so that the motion of one restricts the motion of the other, and the two move on very different time scales, since the inertia and the drag of the water make the USV slow to respond, whereas the UAV responds within a fraction of a second. Wind and waves disturb both vehicles, and the UAV must take off from and land on a deck that moves with the waves. Operating the pair as a single system therefore requires both a model of the tether that accounts for its weight and a coordination in which each vehicle anticipates the motion of the other.

% What does this work propose?
This work addresses the control of a USV and a UAV connected by a tether, so that they carry out maritime missions autonomously as a single system. Each vehicle is driven by its own non-linear model predictive controller (NMPC), and the two controllers exchange the trajectories they predict, so that each keeps the separation within the reach of the tether by taking into account where the other vehicle is going. The controller of the UAV also predicts the force of the hanging tether, whose slack is kept constant by a winch on the deck of the USV, and a disturbance observer compensates the forces that the models do not describe, such as the wind. Simple manoeuvres, such as a takeoff, an orbit or a landing, give the reference of each vehicle, and missions chain them into complete operations. The main contributions are control-oriented models of the simulated USV and UAV for real-time predictive control, two cooperating NMPCs that track their references with an accuracy of a few centimetres, also under wind, and keep the tether within its reach whatever reference they are given, a simulation environment of the tethered system at sea, a set of manoeuvres and missions, and the software of the system, which is publicly available\footnote{\url{https://github.com/tomasgesteves1/tethered-usv-uav-nmpc}}.
