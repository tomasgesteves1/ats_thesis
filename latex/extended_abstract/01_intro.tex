\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

% Why join a USV and a UAV with a tether?
\IEEEPARstart{S}{aving} lives at sea, protecting marine ecosystems and securing maritime infrastructure all rely on a continuous and local awareness of what happens at sea~\cite{europeanmaritimesafetyagencyEMSAOutlook2025_v302025}. Unmanned surface vessels (USVs) keep a presence at sea for long periods at a low cost, but they only see as far as the horizon, while unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) sense from above, but have a short endurance and an unreliable long-range link~\cite{zeng2016uav,kishk2020tethered}. A tether between the two supplies the UAV with power and a reliable communication link from the USV, which removes both limits at once~\cite{kishk2020tethered}, and such systems are already offered by industry~\cite{exail_elistair_tethered,acua_ocean_teledyne_mod}.

% What makes the tethered pair hard to control?
The tether, however, turns the two vehicles into a single system. The distance between them must stay within the reach of the tether, so that the motion of one restricts the motion of the other, and the two move on very different time scales, the USV slowly, dominated by its inertia and by the drag of the water, and the UAV quickly. Wind and waves disturb both vehicles, and the UAV must take off from and land on a deck that moves with the waves. Operating the pair as a single system therefore requires both a model of the tether that accounts for its weight and a coordination in which each vehicle anticipates the motion of the other.

% What does this work propose?
This work addresses the control of a USV, the WAM-V, and a UAV, the x500, connected by a tether, so that they carry out maritime missions autonomously as a single system. Each vehicle is driven by its own non-linear model predictive controller (NMPC), and the two controllers exchange the trajectories they predict, so that each keeps the separation within the reach of the tether by taking into account where the other vehicle is going, rather than where it is. The controller of the UAV also predicts the force of the hanging tether, whose slack is kept constant by a winch on the deck of the USV, and a disturbance observer compensates the forces that the models do not describe, such as the wind. Simple manoeuvres, such as a takeoff, an orbit or a landing, give the reference of each vehicle, and missions chain them into complete operations. The main contributions are a closed-form model of the force of a slack tether that accounts for its weight, the two cooperating NMPCs, manoeuvres for the takeoff from and the landing on the moving deck and for the inspection of a structure by both vehicles, and a simulation of the tethered system at sea in which they are evaluated.
