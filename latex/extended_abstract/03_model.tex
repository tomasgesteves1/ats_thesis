\section{Modelling and Control}
\label{sec:model}

% What must the models capture?
Each controller predicts the motion of its vehicle over a short horizon, so its model must be accurate enough to anticipate that motion, yet simple enough to be evaluated many times within each sampling interval. The models are derived from the physics of each vehicle, and the force of the tether is described by a closed-form model that accounts for its weight.

\subsection{USV Model}

% Which motion of the USV does its controller predict?
The WAM-V moves on the surface, so its controller only needs to predict its motion in the horizontal plane. The state collects its position $(x,y)$ and heading $\psi$ in the inertial frame, and its surge, sway and yaw velocities $(u,v,r)$ in its body frame. The kinematics are $\dot x = u\cos\psi - v\sin\psi$, $\dot y = u\sin\psi + v\cos\psi$ and $\dot\psi = r$, and the dynamics follow from Newton's and Euler's laws in the rotating body frame, with the linear-plus-quadratic hydrodynamic damping of~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \dot u &= \tfrac{1}{m}\big[X - (X_u + X_{uu}^{\pm}|u|)u\big] + vr, \\
    \dot v &= \tfrac{1}{m}\big[Y - (Y_v + Y_{vv}|v|)v\big] - ur, \\
    \dot r &= \tfrac{1}{I_z}\big[N - (N_r + N_{rr}|r|)r\big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:usv_dyn}
\end{equation}
where $m$ and $I_z$ are the mass and the yaw inertia, and the quadratic surge damping $X_{uu}^{\pm}$ may differ between forward and backward motion. The force and moment $(X,Y,N)$ are produced by two azimuth thrusters, with thrusts $T_L, T_R$ and angles $\alpha_L, \alpha_R$, mounted behind the centre of gravity at $(-x_{arm}, \pm y_{arm})$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    X &= T_L\cos\alpha_L + T_R\cos\alpha_R, \\
    Y &= T_L\sin\alpha_L + T_R\sin\alpha_R, \\
    N &= -x_{arm}(T_L\sin\alpha_L + T_R\sin\alpha_R) \\
      &\quad - y_{arm}(T_L\cos\alpha_L - T_R\cos\alpha_R).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:usv_alloc}
\end{equation}

\subsection{UAV Model}

% Which forces move the UAV?
The x500 is described by its position $\mathbf{p} = [x,y,z]^\top$ and velocity in the inertial frame. Its acceleration results from the thrust $T$ along the vertical axis of its body, $\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{B}}$, gravity, a linear rotor drag $d_r$ on the horizontal velocity, and the force of the tether,
\begin{equation}
    \ddot{\mathbf{p}} = \frac{T}{m}\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{B}} - \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\g\end{bmatrix} - \frac{d_r}{m}\begin{bmatrix}\dot x\\ \dot y\\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \frac{1}{m}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{tether}}.
    \label{eq:uav_dyn}
\end{equation}

% How does the UAV respond to the commands of the controller?
The controller does not drive the rotors, but sends attitude and thrust setpoints to the autopilot of the UAV, PX4~\cite{meier2015px4}, which tracks them with its own attitude controller. The attitude therefore follows its command with a delay. The autopilot rotates the UAV so that the direction of the thrust approaches the commanded one, and controls the yaw separately, which is described by two first-order responses, with time constants $\tau$ and $\tau_\psi$, to commands received after transport delays $\tau_d$ and $\tau_{d,\psi}$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \dot{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathcal{B}} &= \tfrac{1}{\tau}\big(\mathbf{R}(\phi_{\mathrm{cmd}},\theta_{\mathrm{cmd}},\psi_{\mathrm{cmd}})\big|_{t-\tau_d}[0,0,1]^\top - \mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{B}}\big), \\
    \dot\psi &= \tfrac{1}{\tau_\psi}\big(\psi_{\mathrm{cmd}}(t-\tau_{d,\psi}) - \psi\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uav_att}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}$ is the rotation of the body for the commanded roll, pitch and yaw. Since the delayed commands are not part of the state, they are approximated to first order by the commands and their rates, $\phi_{\mathrm{cmd}}(t-\tau_d) \approx \phi_{\mathrm{cmd}} - \tau_d\,\dot\phi_{\mathrm{cmd}}$, and likewise for the pitch and the yaw.

\subsection{Tether Force Model}

% How large is the force of a slack tether?
The tether runs from the UAV to a winch on the deck of the USV, at $\mathbf{p}_w = [x_v, y_v, z_t]^\top$, above the centre of gravity of the USV at a fixed height $z_t$. The winch keeps the length $L$ of the tether slightly longer than the distance $d = \|\mathbf{r}\|$ between its ends, $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p}_w - \mathbf{p}$, so that its slack ratio $\varepsilon_0 = (L-d)/L$ stays constant. A tether of linear mass $\mu$ then hangs as a catenary, and the tension at the UAV is close to the weight of the tether, $\mu g L$, so that it grows in proportion to the distance,
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm{tether}} = c_d\,d, \qquad c_d = \frac{\mu g}{1-\varepsilon_0}.
    \label{eq:tether_tension}
\end{equation}

% In which direction does it pull?
The weight of the tether also turns its force away from the chord, the straight line $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_0 = \mathbf{r}/d$ towards the winch, by an angle $\gamma$ towards the vertical, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:tether_plane}. Only the weight normal to the chord bends the tether, so the deflection is largest for a horizontal chord, where it equals the angle $\gamma_0$ of the catenary at its end, and vanishes for a vertical one. It is therefore taken as $\gamma = \gamma_0\cos\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the depression angle of the chord below the horizontal direction $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_h$ towards the winch, and the force on the UAV is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{tether}} = c_d\,d\,\big[\cos(\alpha+\gamma)\,\hat{\mathbf{e}}_h - \sin(\alpha+\gamma)\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{I}}\big],
    \label{eq:tether_force}
\end{equation}
a closed-form expression of the positions of the two vehicles, which replaces the numerical solution of the catenary at every step of the prediction.

\subsection{Cooperative NMPC}

% Which problem does each controller solve?
Each vehicle is driven by its own NMPC, which solves, at every sampling instant $k$, an optimal control problem over a horizon of $N$ steps,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \min_{\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{u}},\hat{\boldsymbol\sigma}} \;& \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\ell(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i},\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}) + \ell_f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{N}) + \sum_{i,j} \rho(\hat\sigma_{j,i}) \\
    \text{s.t.}\;& \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0} = \mathbf{x}_k, \quad \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i+1} = \boldsymbol\Phi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i},\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}), \\
    & \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}\in\mathcal{X}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\in\mathcal{U}, \quad h_j(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \le \hat\sigma_{j,i}, \quad \hat\sigma_{j,i}\ge 0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ocp}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\Phi$ integrates the model over one sampling interval, the stage cost $\ell$ and the terminal cost $\ell_f$ penalise the deviation from the reference, and only the first input is applied before the problem is solved again. The limits that the vehicle cannot exceed, $\mathcal{X}$ and $\mathcal{U}$, are hard, whereas the constraints $h_j$ on states that a disturbance can push beyond their bounds are softened by slacks $\sigma_j$, penalised by $\rho(\sigma) = \mu_1\sigma + \mu_2\sigma^2$, so that the problem always has a solution and a constraint is violated only when it cannot be met. Since the constraints act on the predicted trajectory, the controller keeps the vehicle within its limits even when the reference itself cannot be followed, so that the reference can be as simple as a single point.

% Why two controllers, and how do they cooperate?
A single controller for both vehicles would impose one horizon and one sampling time on vehicles that move on very different time scales, and enlarge the problem to be solved within each sampling interval. Each vehicle therefore has its own NMPC, and the two exchange the trajectories they predict, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}. Each controller resamples the latest prediction of the other at the nodes of its own horizon, and constrains the separation between the vehicles along it, so that it anticipates where the other vehicle is going. The horizon of the UAV is shorter than that of the USV, so the USV enforces this constraint only over the part of its horizon that the prediction of the UAV covers, a window that slides forward as the problem is solved again at every instant.

% What does the NMPC of the USV optimise and respect?
\textit{USV NMPC.} To bound both the thrusts and how fast they change, the thrusts and angles become part of the state, $\mathbf{x}_v = [x,y,\psi,u,v,r,T_L,T_R,\alpha_L,\alpha_R]^\top$, and the inputs are their rates, so that their ranges act on the state and their rate limits on the input. The stage cost penalises the errors of position, heading and velocity, together with the thrusts and their rates. A soft lower bound on the surge speed keeps the USV sailing bow first, and the separation from the predicted trajectory of the UAV, $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_a$, is limited to the reach of the tether, $d_{max}$,
\begin{equation}
    (x-\hat x_a)^2 + (y-\hat y_a)^2 + (\hat z_a - z_t)^2 - d_{max}^2 \le \sigma.
    \label{eq:usv_tether}
\end{equation}

% What does the NMPC of the UAV optimise and respect?
\textit{UAV NMPC.} In the same way, the commanded attitude and thrust become part of the state, and the inputs $\mathbf{u}_a$ are their rates. The prediction model combines \eqref{eq:uav_dyn}--\eqref{eq:tether_force}, with the winch placed on the predicted trajectory of the USV. The stage cost
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \ell = {} & \|\mathbf{p}-\mathbf{p}_{ref}\|^2_{Q_p} + \|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{v}_{ref}\|^2_{Q_v} + \big\|[\phi,\theta]^\top\big\|^2_{Q_{tilt}} \\
    & + q_\psi\big(1 - w_b\cos(\psi-\psi_{ref})\big) \\
    & + Q_T\,(T-mg)^2 + \|\mathbf{u}_a\|^2_R
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uav_cost}
\end{equation}
penalises the errors of position and velocity, the tilt, the deviation of the thrust from its hover value and the rates of the commands. It also turns the UAV towards a point of interest, such as the winch, a structure or a target, whose bearing is $\psi_{ref}$, with a weight $w_b = \|\mathbf{b}\|/\sqrt{\|\mathbf{b}\|^2+\delta}$, where $\mathbf{b}$ is the horizontal vector to that point, which fades to zero right above it, where the bearing is undefined. Soft constraints bound the height above the water, the horizontal and vertical speeds and the tilt, the angle between the thrust and the vertical,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    & z \ge z_{min}, && \big\|[\dot x,\dot y]^\top\big\| \le v_{xy,max}, \\
    & |\dot z| \le v_{z,max}, && \cos\phi\cos\theta \ge \cos\vartheta_{max},
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq:uav_bounds}
\end{equation}
and the tilt is limited both on the attitude and on its command, so that the setpoint sent to the autopilot also respects it. The tether adds two more. The separation is limited, $d \le d_{max}$, and so is the rate at which it changes, the projection of the relative velocity $\dot{\mathbf{r}}$ of the winch onto the chord,
\begin{equation}
    |\dot d| = \frac{|\mathbf{r}^\top\dot{\mathbf{r}}|}{d} \le \dot d_{max},
    \label{eq:uav_ddot}
\end{equation}
which may not exceed the speed at which the winch spools the tether. Only the radial motion is restricted, so that the UAV remains free to move around the winch at a constant distance.

% How are unmodelled forces compensated?
\textit{Disturbance observer.} A force that the model does not describe, such as the wind, makes the prediction drift from the true motion and leaves a steady offset. Following the disturbance-model approach to offset-free predictive control~\cite{pannocchia2003offsetfree,maeder2009offsetfree}, an external force $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathrm{ext}}$, held constant over the horizon, is added to \eqref{eq:uav_dyn} and estimated at every instant from the one-step prediction error of the velocity,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathrm{ext},k} = \mathrm{sat}_{\bar F}\Big(\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathrm{ext},k-1} + \beta\frac{m}{T_s}\big(\mathbf{v}_k - \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k|k-1}\big)\Big),
    \label{eq:observer}
\end{equation}
where $T_s$ is the sampling time, the gain $\beta$ sets how quickly the estimate follows the force, and the saturation $\bar F$ bounds it.
