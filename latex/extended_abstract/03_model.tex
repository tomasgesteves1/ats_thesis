\section{Modelling and Control}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{USV Model}

% Which motion of the USV must its controller predict?
The controller of the USV only needs to predict its motion on the surface, so the WAM-V is described in the horizontal plane, by its position $(x,y)$ and heading $\psi$ in the inertial frame and by its surge, sway and yaw velocities $(u,v,r)$ in its body frame, while heave, roll and pitch are left as disturbances of the waves. The kinematics are $\dot x = u\cos\psi - v\sin\psi$, $\dot y = u\sin\psi + v\cos\psi$ and $\dot\psi = r$, and the dynamics follow from Newton's and Euler's laws in the rotating body frame, with the linear-plus-quadratic hydrodynamic damping of~\cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011},
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \dot u &= \tfrac{1}{m}\big[X - (X_u + X_{uu}^{\pm}|u|)u\big] + vr, \\
    \dot v &= \tfrac{1}{m}\big[Y - (Y_v + Y_{vv}|v|)v\big] - ur, \\
    \dot r &= \tfrac{1}{I_z}\big[N - (N_r + N_{rr}|r|)r\big],
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:usv_dyn}
\end{equation}
where the quadratic surge damping $X_{uu}^{\pm}$ differs between forward and backward motion. The forces and moment $(X,Y,N)$ are produced by two azimuth thrusters, with thrusts $T_L, T_R$ and angles $\alpha_L, \alpha_R$, mounted at $(-x_{arm}, \pm y_{arm})$,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    X &= T_L\cos\alpha_L + T_R\cos\alpha_R, \\
    Y &= T_L\sin\alpha_L + T_R\sin\alpha_R, \\
    N &= -x_{arm}(T_L\sin\alpha_L + T_R\sin\alpha_R) \\
      &\quad - y_{arm}(T_L\cos\alpha_L - T_R\cos\alpha_R).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:usv_alloc}
\end{equation}

\subsection{UAV Model}

% Which forces move the UAV?
The x500 is described by its position $\mathbf{p} = [x,y,z]^\top$ and attitude $(\phi,\theta,\psi)$. Its acceleration results from the thrust $T$ along its body vertical axis $\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{B}}$, gravity, a linear rotor drag $d_r$ on the horizontal velocity, and the force of the tether,
\begin{equation}
    \ddot{\mathbf{p}} = \frac{T}{m}\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{B}} - \begin{bmatrix}0\\0\\g\end{bmatrix} - \frac{d_r}{m}\begin{bmatrix}\dot x\\ \dot y\\ 0\end{bmatrix} + \frac{1}{m}\mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{tether}}.
    \label{eq:uav_dyn}
\end{equation}

% How does the UAV respond to the commands of the controller?
The UAV is driven through its autopilot, PX4~\cite{meier2015px4}, at the attitude level, so the controller does not act on the attitude directly but on the setpoint of the autopilot. The direction of the thrust follows the commanded one as a first-order system with time constant $\tau$, after a transport delay $T_t$, and the yaw follows its command with $\tau_\psi$ after the same delay,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \dot{\mathbf{z}}^{\mathcal{B}} &= \tfrac{1}{\tau}\big(\mathbf{R}(\phi_{\mathrm{cmd}},\theta_{\mathrm{cmd}},\psi_{\mathrm{cmd}})|_{t-T_t}[0,0,1]^\top - \mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{B}}\big), \\
    \dot\psi &= \tfrac{1}{\tau_\psi}\big(\psi_{\mathrm{cmd}}(t-T_t) - \psi\big),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:uav_att}
\end{equation}
while the thrust reaches the rotors after a shorter delay $T_T$. In the prediction, each delay is represented by a first-order Pad\'e approximation, which adds one state per delayed command and, unlike a Taylor expansion, never lets a command act on the vehicle before its delay has elapsed.

\subsection{Tether Force Model}

% How large is the force of a slack tether?
The tether is attached to a winch on the USV, at $\mathbf{p}_v$, which keeps its length $L$ slightly longer than the distance $d = \|\mathbf{r}\|$ between its ends, $\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{p}_v - \mathbf{p}$, with a constant slack ratio $\varepsilon_0 = (L-d)/L$. A tether of linear mass $\mu$ then hangs as a catenary, and the tension at the UAV is close to the weight of the tether, $T_{\mathrm{tether}} \approx \mu g L$, so that
\begin{equation}
    T_{\mathrm{tether}} = c_d\,d, \qquad c_d = \frac{\mu g}{1-\varepsilon_0}.
    \label{eq:tether_tension}
\end{equation}

% In which direction does it pull?
The weight of the tether also turns its force away from the chord $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_0 = \mathbf{r}/d$, towards the vertical, by an angle $\gamma$. Since only the weight normal to the chord bends the tether, this deflection is largest for a horizontal chord, where it equals the angle $\gamma_0$ of the catenary at its ends, and vanishes for a vertical one, so it is taken as $\gamma = \gamma_0\cos\alpha$, where $\alpha$ is the depression angle of the chord below the horizontal direction $\hat{\mathbf{e}}_h$ in the plane of the catenary. The force on the UAV is then
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{\mathrm{tether}} = c_d\,d\,\big[\cos(\alpha+\gamma)\,\hat{\mathbf{e}}_h - \sin(\alpha+\gamma)\,\mathbf{z}^{\mathcal{I}}\big],
    \label{eq:tether_force}
\end{equation}
a closed-form expression of the positions of the two vehicles, smooth for every orientation of the chord, which replaces the numerical solution of the catenary at every step of the controller.

\subsection{Cooperative NMPC}

% Which problem does each controller solve?
Each vehicle is driven by its own NMPC, which solves, at every sampling instant $k$, an optimal control problem over a horizon of $N$ steps,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \min_{\hat{\mathbf{x}},\hat{\mathbf{u}},\hat{\boldsymbol\sigma}} \;& \sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\ell(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i},\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}) + \ell_f(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{N}) + \sum_{i} \rho(\hat{\boldsymbol\sigma}_{i}) \\
    \text{s.t.}\;& \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{0} = \mathbf{x}_k, \quad \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i+1} = \boldsymbol\Phi(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i},\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}), \\
    & \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{i}\in\mathcal{U}, \quad h_j(\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{i}) \le \hat\sigma_{j,i}, \quad \hat\sigma_{j,i}\ge 0,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:ocp}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol\Phi$ integrates the model over one sampling interval with a fourth-order Runge--Kutta method. The limits of the inputs are hard, whereas the state constraints are softened by slacks $\sigma_j$, penalised by $\rho(\sigma) = \mu_1\sigma + \mu_2\sigma^2$, so that the problem always has a solution, with penalties large enough to keep the violations close to zero whenever the constraints can be met. Both problems are written in CasADi~\cite{anderssonCasADiSoftwareFramework2019} and solved by acados~\cite{verschueren2022acados}.

% What happens when the reference cannot be reached?
A reference far from the vehicle, or beyond what its limits or the tether allow, must not drive it against its constraints. The position error therefore enters both costs through a pseudo-Huber function, $\mathbf{e}\,(1+\|\mathbf{e}\|^2/\delta^2)^{-1/4}$ as a residual, which is quadratic near the reference and grows only linearly beyond a distance $\delta$. A distant reference thus pulls the vehicle with a bounded force, which never outweighs the penalty of a constraint, and the vehicle approaches it as closely as its limits allow.

% Why two controllers, and how do they cooperate?
A single controller for both vehicles would impose one horizon and one sampling time on vehicles that move on very different time scales, and enlarge the problem to be solved in real time. Each vehicle therefore has its own NMPC, and the two exchange the trajectories they predict, as shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}. Each controller then constrains the distance between the vehicles along the predicted trajectory of the other, so that it anticipates where the other vehicle is going, rather than where it is.

% What does the NMPC of the USV optimise and respect?
\textit{USV NMPC.} To limit both the thrusts and their rates, the thrusts and angles are included in the state, $\mathbf{x}_v = [x,y,\psi,u,v,r,T_L,T_R,\alpha_L,\alpha_R]^\top$, and the inputs are their rates, bounded by the limits of the thrusters. The stage cost penalises the errors of position, heading and velocity, together with the thrusts and their rates. A soft lower bound on the surge speed limits the motion astern, for which the hull and the thrusters are least effective, and the separation from the predicted trajectory of the UAV, $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_a$, is limited by the reach of the tether,
\begin{equation}
    (x-\hat x_a)^2 + (y-\hat y_a)^2 + (\hat z_a - z_t)^2 - d_{max}^2 \le \sigma,
    \label{eq:usv_tether}
\end{equation}
where $z_t$ is the height of the winch.

% What does the NMPC of the UAV optimise and respect?
\textit{UAV NMPC.} The commanded attitude and thrust are included in the state, together with the states of the delays, and the inputs are their rates. The model combines \eqref{eq:uav_dyn}--\eqref{eq:tether_force}, with the tether attached to the predicted trajectory of the USV. The stage cost penalises the errors of position and velocity, the tilt, the deviation of the thrust from hover and the inputs, and keeps the UAV looking at the winch. The soft constraints bound the height, the horizontal and vertical speeds and the tilt from the vertical, $\cos\phi\cos\theta \ge \cos\phi_{max}$, which is imposed on both the attitude and its command, so that the setpoint sent to the autopilot also respects it. The tether adds two more. The separation is limited, $d \le d_{max}$, and so is the rate at which it grows, $\dot d \le \dot d_{max}$, which may not exceed the pay-out speed of the winch.

% How are unmodelled forces compensated?
\textit{Disturbance observer.} A force that the model does not describe, such as wind, makes the prediction drift and leaves a steady offset. Following the disturbance-model approach to offset-free predictive control~\cite{pannocchia2003offsetfree,maeder2009offsetfree}, an external force $\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathrm{ext}}$, held constant over the horizon, is added to \eqref{eq:uav_dyn} and estimated from the one-step prediction error of the velocity,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathrm{ext},k} = \mathrm{sat}_{\bar F}\Big(\hat{\mathbf{F}}_{\mathrm{ext},k-1} + \beta\frac{m}{T_s}\big(\mathbf{v}_k - \hat{\mathbf{v}}_{k|k-1}\big)\Big),
    \label{eq:observer}
\end{equation}
where the gain $\beta$ sets how quickly the estimate follows the force, with a time constant of about $T_s/\beta$, and the saturation $\bar F$ keeps it within the forces the UAV can expect.
