\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:backg}

% How is the tether usually represented?
\textit{Tether models.} In tethered aerial systems, the tether is most often modelled as massless, inextensible and taut, so that it acts along the straight line between its ends, as in the buoy--UAV system of~\cite{kouraniMarineLocomotionTethered2021} and the cooperative manipulation of~\cite{novakCollaborativeObjectManipulation2024}. Alternatively, its effect is not modelled and is lumped into a disturbance estimated online and rejected by the controller~\cite{yuReducedComplexityActiveDisturbance2023}. Neither describes a slack tether, whose weight bends it into a catenary~\cite{irvine1981cable} and turns its force away from the straight line.

% How are the two vehicles controlled together?
\textit{Control and cooperation.} Predictive control is the common choice for the cooperation of a UAV and a USV, since it can state the coupling between them as constraints over a horizon. In~\cite{liNMPCbasedUAVUSVCooperative2023}, an NMPC drives a UAV to follow and land on a moving USV, but only the UAV is controlled and the interaction is unidirectional. In~\cite{zhangCooperativeControlFramework2025}, the USV follows a predefined path with a line-of-sight guidance law, and the UAV keeps a relative configuration with its own NMPC, in a leader--follower structure in which the motion of the USV ignores the UAV. In~\cite{novakCollaborativeObjectManipulation2024}, a single, centralised MPC plans the motion of both vehicles jointly, but imposes one horizon and one sampling time on two vehicles whose dynamics evolve on very different time scales. Either one vehicle plans without regard to the other, or both are planned as one.

% What does this work do differently?
This work addresses each of these points. It models the force of a slack tether in closed form, accounting for its weight, and compensates what the model leaves out with a disturbance observer. Each vehicle is driven by its own NMPC, with a horizon and a sampling time suited to its dynamics, and the two controllers exchange their predicted trajectories, so that each plans its own motion online while anticipating that of the other, within the reach of the tether.
