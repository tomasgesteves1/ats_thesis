\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:backg}

% How can the tether be represented?
\textit{Tether models.} A heavy tether suspended between two points takes the shape of a catenary, whose sag and tension follow from the equations of static equilibrium~\cite{irvine1981cable}. Such quasistatic models are inexpensive, but neglect the dynamics of the tether, which lumped-mass models capture by discretising it into a chain of nodes joined by spring--damper segments, as in MoorDyn~\cite{hall2015moordyn}, at a higher computational cost. In tethered and cooperative UAV systems, the tether is often simplified further, either modelled as massless and always taut, so that it pulls along the straight line between its ends, as in the buoy--UAV system of Kourani and Daher~\cite{kouraniMarineLocomotionTethered2021} and the cooperative manipulation of Nov\'{a}k et al.~\cite{novakCollaborativeObjectManipulation2024}, or treated as an unknown disturbance, estimated online and rejected by the controller~\cite{yuReducedComplexityActiveDisturbance2023}. Neither describes a slack tether, whose weight turns its force away from that straight line.

% How are the two vehicles coordinated?
\textit{Control and cooperation.} Predictive control is a common choice for UAV--USV systems, since it can state the coupling between the vehicles as constraints over a prediction horizon. Li et al.~\cite{liNMPCbasedUAVUSVCooperative2023} use an NMPC to drive a UAV to track and land on a moving USV, but only the UAV is controlled, and the USV moves independently. Zhang et al.~\cite{zhangCooperativeControlFramework2025} adopt a leader--follower structure, in which the USV follows a predefined path and the UAV keeps a desired position relative to it with its own NMPC. Nov\'{a}k et al.~\cite{novakCollaborativeObjectManipulation2024} compute the commands of both vehicles in a single, centralised problem, with tethers modelled as massless and taut. In each case, either the tether is simplified or the coordination runs in one direction only, with one vehicle adapting to the other without being anticipated by it.
