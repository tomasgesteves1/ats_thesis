\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}

This work developed the cooperative control of a USV and a UAV connected by a tether. Models of both vehicles, validated against the simulator within $1\,\%$ and $2.5\,\%$ of the range of their states over the horizons of the controllers, and a closed-form model of the force of a slack tether, more than $70\,\%$ more accurate than a taut tether or a hanging load, are used by two NMPCs, one for each vehicle, which exchange their predicted trajectories to keep the tether within its reach. The results show that the NMPC of the UAV tracks a reference with less than a quarter of the error of the position controller of PX4, that both controllers plan, from single points or unreachable references, motions within the limits of both vehicles and of the tether, and that the two vehicles carry out missions as one system, as when the USV supports the UAV around a tower that the tether alone could not reach. The UAV lands on the moving deck within a few centimetres of the centre of the pad under waves of up to $0.44\,\text{m}$, and, with the disturbance observer, under winds of up to $12\,\text{m/s}$, which without it prevent the mission.

The system was evaluated in simulation only, with the state of the vehicles taken from the simulator, and the next step is to test it with the real vehicles. The results also point to the takeoff in strong wind as the most exposed moment, to be addressed by the way the UAV leaves the deck rather than by its controller, and to obstacles, which could be added to both NMPCs as further constraints, on the vehicles and on the tether. Finally, the missions are fixed sequences of manoeuvres, and missions that branch, for instance when a target is lost, would make the system ready for real operations.
