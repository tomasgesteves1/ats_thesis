\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}

% What was achieved?
This work set out to operate a USV and a UAV connected by a tether as a single system at sea, each vehicle within its own limits and both within the reach of the tether. Models of both vehicles, derived from their physics and validated against the simulator without any fitting, predict their motion over the horizons of the controllers with errors of a few percent, and a closed-form model of a slack tether that accounts for its weight reduces the error of its force by about three quarters relative to a taut tether or a hanging load. On these models, each vehicle is driven by its own NMPC, and the two exchange the trajectories they predict, so that each keeps the separation within the reach of the tether while anticipating where the other vehicle is going.

% What do the results show?
By anticipating the motion instead of reacting to the error, the controller of the UAV tracks a trajectory with an error more than four times smaller than that of the position controller of PX4, and, when a reference demands more than a vehicle can deliver, each controller gives up following it only as far as the limits require, so that the manoeuvres can remain simple. The UAV lands on the moving deck a few centimetres from the centre of the pad in every sea tested, and, with the disturbance observer, also under wind, which otherwise forces the landing to be aborted. Coupled by the tether, the two vehicles complete an inspection, a patrol and a recovery as a single system, and in the inspection the tether becomes not a constraint to be avoided, but a means to survey a tower that neither vehicle could survey alone.

% What comes next?
The system was evaluated in simulation only, and the next step is to test it with the real vehicles, where the state is estimated from noisy sensors, the communication between the vehicles has delays, and the models must be validated again. A prediction of the motion of the deck could time the touchdown to when the deck moves down, and the takeoff remains to be tested under wind. Finally, missions that branch, for instance when a target is lost, and obstacles added to both NMPCs as constraints on the vehicles and on the tether, would bring the system closer to real operations.
