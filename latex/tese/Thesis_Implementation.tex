\chapter{Implementation}
\label{chapter:sys_implementation}

Validating the cooperative \gls{nmpc} architecture developed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:control} requires a platform where the USV, the UAV, and the tether connecting them can be exercised under realistic and repeatable conditions. Testing directly on physical hardware is costly, slow to iterate on, and exposes the vehicles to damage whenever a controller is misconfigured or a new maneuver is tried for the first time. A simulation environment removes these constraints: controllers can be developed and debugged through many fast iterations, without waiting on hardware availability or risking the platforms themselves.

Simulation also makes it possible to evaluate performance under conditions that are difficult, dangerous, or simply impractical to reproduce physically. Scenarios such as strong wind gusts, one controller failing to find a feasible solution, or the \gls{uav} being unable to keep pace with the \gls{usv}, can be run as many times as needed, with the exact same initial conditions and disturbances, something field testing cannot guarantee due to the inherent variability of a real environment. This repeatability is essential for a fair comparison between controllers or parameter choices, since any difference observed can be attributed to the change being tested rather than to uncontrolled environmental variation. Every state of the system is also available exactly, without the sensor noise or estimation error present in a physical setup, which allows the behaviour of the controllers themselves to be isolated and analyzed independently of state estimation.

Beyond validating the control strategy, the simulated system is also used to develop and test the surrounding software architecture: the communication between the two \gls{nmpc} controllers, the coordination of maneuvers, and the central dashboard that monitors and manages the mission. Building this environment on \gls{ros} and Gazebo allows the same middleware and software structure used here to carry over to a future deployment on physical hardware, reducing the gap between the simulated and the real system.

This chapter details the practical implementation of the cooperative system across five main areas: the simulation environment (Section~\ref{sec:simulation_environment}), system identification (Section~\ref{sec:system_identification}), controller implementation and solver tuning (Section~\ref{sec:controller_implementation}), coordinated maneuvers and mission logic (Section~\ref{sec:maneuvers_and_missions}), and the underlying software architecture and operator dashboard (Section~\ref{sec:software_architecture_and_dashboard}).
