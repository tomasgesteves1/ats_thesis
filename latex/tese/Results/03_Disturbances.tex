\section{Disturbances}
\label{sec:res_disturbances}

% Which disturbances, and which manoeuvre?
The previous results were obtained on a calm sea and without wind. The two disturbances are now added in turn, during the manoeuvre most exposed to them, the landing on the deck of the moving \gls{usv}. In each run, the \gls{uav} takes off while the \gls{usv} starts a route at $0.75\,\text{m/s}$, moves away from it, and lands back on the pad, in the phases described in Section~\ref{subsec:maneuver_primitives}.

\subsection{Waves}
\label{subsec:res_waves}

% How rough is the sea, and how does it move the deck?
The waves are set by the gain of the wave field of the simulator, from $0$, a calm sea, to $2$. With a gain of $1$, the winch on the deck rises and falls by $0.25\,\text{m}$ about its mean height, and with a gain of $2$ by $0.44\,\text{m}$, with a period of about $5\,\text{s}$, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_waves}. The deck also rolls and pitches, which moves the winch sideways as well.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Results/figures/03_disturbances/waves_landing.pdf}
    \caption[Landing with waves.]{Landing with waves, from the start of the descent to the touchdown: height of the winch, height of the \gls{uav} above it, and horizontal offset of the \gls{uav} from it.}
    \label{fig:res_waves}
\end{figure}

% Does the UAV stay above the pad while the deck moves?
The landing is not affected by the heave in its timing. It takes between $25$ and $27\,\text{s}$ for the three sea states, and the \gls{uav} descends in the same way, since its reference is taken above the mean height of the winch and does not follow the deck up and down. The horizontal offset from the winch stays within $5\,\text{cm}$ during the descent on a calm sea and with a gain of $1$, and within $10\,\text{cm}$ with a gain of $2$, as the deck rolls under the \gls{uav}. The touchdown is $0$, $2$ and $4\,\text{cm}$ from the centre of the pad.

% What does the heave change, then?
What the heave changes is the contact. The final approach aims at a point below the deck, so that the \gls{uav} touches down even if the deck falls away from it. The speed of the contact is then set by the motion of the deck, and grows with the waves, from $0.15\,\text{m/s}$ on a calm sea to $0.26$ and $0.39\,\text{m/s}$ with gains of $1$ and $2$. In every case, the winch then holds the \gls{uav} against the pad, and it remains there as the deck keeps moving.

\subsection{Wind}
\label{subsec:res_wind}

% How is the wind applied?
The wind acts on the \gls{uav} as a horizontal drag force, proportional to the square of its speed, along a fixed direction, across the first leg of the route of the \gls{usv}, with gusts of $\pm 30\,\%$ that vary over periods of $7$ to $23\,\text{s}$. Winds of $5$, $8$ and $12\,\text{m/s}$ give mean forces of $0.9$, $2.4$ and $5.3\,\text{N}$, the last a quarter of the weight of the \gls{uav}. Each wind was flown twice, with and without the disturbance observer, since this is the force the observer is meant to compensate.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Results/figures/03_disturbances/wind.pdf}
    \caption[Landing with wind.]{Landing with wind: (a) horizontal offset of the \gls{uav} from the winch when the descent starts, with and without the disturbance observer, and (b) force applied by a wind of $12\,\text{m/s}$ and the force estimated by the observer, from the takeoff to the touchdown.}
    \label{fig:res_wind}
\end{figure}

% What happens without the observer?
Without the observer, the \gls{nmpc} only knows the forces of its model, and the wind pushes the \gls{uav} away from its reference until the error in position balances it. Above the pad, this offset grows with the wind, from $18\,\text{cm}$ at $5\,\text{m/s}$ to $44\,\text{cm}$ at $8\,\text{m/s}$, Figure~\ref{fig:res_wind}a. The landing waits for the \gls{uav} to be centred above the pad before it descends, which never happens, and the descent starts only once this wait has timed out, so that the landing takes more than twice as long. The pull of the winch then brings the \gls{uav} down onto the pad, $7\,\text{cm}$ from its centre. At $12\,\text{m/s}$, the \gls{uav} did not even take off. While it climbed off the pad, still low and pulled by the winch, the wind pushed it off the deck and into the water.

% And with the observer?
With the observer, the offset above the pad stays between $4$ and $8\,\text{cm}$ for the three winds, the landing takes the same $26$ to $28\,\text{s}$ as without wind, and the \gls{uav} touches down $1$ to $4\,\text{cm}$ from the centre of the pad. Figure~\ref{fig:res_wind}b shows why. The estimate settles within a few seconds of the takeoff on the mean force of the wind, but does not follow its gusts, which change faster than the observer responds. The observer turns a wind of $12\,\text{m/s}$, which without it prevents the mission altogether, into a disturbance of a few centimetres.
