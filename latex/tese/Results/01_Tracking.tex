\section{Trajectory Tracking}
\label{sec:res_tracking}

% What is tested in this section?
Evaluated on its own vehicle, each controller faces two distinct questions. The first is how closely it tracks a trajectory that the vehicle can follow, which depends on how well it predicts the motion of the vehicle. The second is how it behaves when the trajectory demands more than the vehicle can deliver, since the controller must then give up following the reference in order to keep the vehicle within its limits.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\gls{usv} Controller}{USV Controller}}
\label{subsec:res_tracking_usv}

% Under which conditions is the USV controller evaluated?
The \gls{usv} controller is evaluated with the \gls{uav} resting on the deck, so that the \gls{usv} moves alone and its response depends only on its own controller.

\subsubsection{Tracking Accuracy}

% Which reference, and why this one?
The reference is a figure-eight sailed twice, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_scenario}. Its two circles, of $12\,\text{m}$ radius, are turned in opposite directions and joined by straight segments, so that the \gls{usv} turns both ways and alternates between turning and sailing straight.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics{Results/figures/01_tracking/usv_track_top.pdf}
    \caption{Top view of the reference figure-eight of the \gls{usv} and path sailed.}
    \label{fig:res_usv_scenario}
\end{figure}

% Is the reference within the limits of the USV?
The reference lies within the capability of the \gls{usv}. The thrust and the azimuth computed by the controller remain far from the limits of the thrusters throughout, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_effort}, and neither their rates nor the surge speed approach their own limits. The \gls{usv} turns mainly through the difference between the thrusts of its two thrusters, which reverses between the two circles, while the azimuth changes by only a few degrees. The tracking error therefore reflects the controller alone, and not the limits of the \gls{usv}.

\end{figure}

% How closely does the USV follow it?
The heading error of the \gls{usv} reaches about $4^\circ$ at the start of the run, while the \gls{usv} accelerates from rest, and decays within the first seconds, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_accuracy}. Thereafter, the heading error remains below one degree, and the position error is of the order of one percent of the length of the \gls{usv}, as listed in Table~\ref{tab:res_usv}. At the scale of the manoeuvre, the path sailed is therefore indistinguishable from the reference, with which it coincides in Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_scenario}.

\subsubsection{Operating Limits}

% Which reference, and why this one?
The second reference asks the \gls{usv} for motions it cannot perform, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_limits_top}, where the \gls{usv} is drawn at a few instants and the dashed outlines mark the poses held by the reference. It begins with a straight segment at $3\,\text{m/s}$, beyond the top speed of the \gls{usv}, which ends in a sharp turn of $90^\circ$ at that speed. It then comes to rest at a point, which is moved first $15\,\text{m}$ backwards and then $15\,\text{m}$ to the left of the \gls{usv}, keeping the same heading.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics{Results/figures/01_tracking/usv_limits_top.pdf}
    \caption{Top view of the reference beyond the limits of the \gls{usv} and path sailed.}
    \label{fig:res_usv_limits_top}
\end{figure}

% What happens on the straight segment and the turn?
On the straight segment, both thrusters reach their maximum thrust, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_limits_inputs}, and the \gls{usv} settles at its top speed of $2.3\,\text{m/s}$, falling behind the reference. The reference has therefore already turned when the \gls{usv} approaches the corner, and the \gls{nmpc} cuts the corner towards it. The \gls{usv} turns mainly through the difference between the thrusts, with the outer thruster at its maximum and the azimuth below $10^\circ$, and, still close to its top speed, crosses the new segment and goes beyond it by about $6\,\text{m}$. To return to the point held by the reference, the \gls{nmpc} then turns both azimuths towards their limit at the maximum rate and reverses one thruster at its maximum rate, so that the \gls{usv} slows down and turns at the same time.

% What happens when the point moves backwards and to the side?
When the point moves backwards, the speed is bounded by the soft lower limit on the surge speed, and the \gls{usv} reverses in a straight line at exactly this limit, as shown in the last panel of Figure~\ref{fig:res_usv_limits_inputs}. While the surge speed rests on this limit, the thrust oscillates by a few newtons about the value that holds it there, without any visible effect on the motion of the \gls{usv}. To reach the point on its left, the \gls{nmpc} turns the \gls{usv} towards it, sails to it and turns back to the heading of the reference.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics{Results/figures/01_tracking/usv_limits_inputs.pdf}
    \caption{Thrust and azimuth of both thrusters of the \gls{usv}, their rates, and its surge speed, against their limits.}
    \label{fig:res_usv_limits_inputs}
\end{figure}


% Are the limits respected?
Throughout the test, the limits of the \gls{usv} are reached but never exceeded, including the soft limit on the surge speed. The controller therefore gives up following the reference only as far as the limits of the \gls{usv} require, and uses its full capability in doing so.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\gls{uav} Controller}{UAV Controller}}
\label{subsec:res_tracking_uav}

% Under which conditions is the UAV controller evaluated?
The \gls{uav} controller is evaluated with the \gls{uav} tethered to the \gls{usv}, which holds its position at the centre of each reference, so that the response of the \gls{uav} depends only on its own controller and on the force of the tether.

\subsubsection{Tracking Accuracy}

% Which reference, and why this one?
The reference is a closed trajectory around the \gls{usv}, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_scenario}. It consists of two fast arcs, along which the separation between the vehicles is constant, joined by two slow turns, along which this separation varies. The \gls{uav} therefore flies at both high and low speed, under a tether force that changes along each lap, since this force grows with the length of tether suspended between the vehicles.

\pagebreak
% What is the NMPC compared with?
As a baseline, the same trajectory is tracked by the position controller supplied with PX4. At each instant, this controller receives the current point of the trajectory and acts on the error between this point and the current position of the \gls{uav}, whereas the \gls{nmpc} receives the trajectory over a one-second horizon and predicts the response of the \gls{uav} and of the tether. The comparison therefore quantifies the benefit of prediction.

% Is the reference within the limits of the UAV?
The trajectory keeps both controllers well within the limits of the \gls{uav}, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_commands}. The commanded tilt stays below $9^\circ$, against a limit of $28.6^\circ$, and the commanded thrust varies by less than $2\,\text{N}$ about its value in hover. Both controllers command nearly the same tilt and thrust along the lap, since both must produce the accelerations that the trajectory requires. The difference between the two results therefore reflects only the tracking performance of each controller, and not a larger control effort or the limits of the \gls{uav}.

% How closely do the two follow it?
Over a full lap, the paths of both controllers are nearly indistinguishable from the reference, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_track}. Their errors are of the order of centimetres, and over three laps the error of the \gls{nmpc} is more than four times smaller than that of PX4, both on average and at its maximum, as listed in Table~\ref{tab:res_uav}.

% Why does the error of PX4 grow with the speed?
The error of PX4 is not uniform along the lap. It increases on each fast arc and decreases on the slow turns, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_error}, and lies mostly along the path. On the fast arcs, PX4 trails the reference by about $80\,\text{ms}$, and a constant lag produces an error proportional to the speed. The \gls{nmpc}, which receives the trajectory ahead and predicts the response of the \gls{uav}, shows no such lag.

\subsubsection{Operating Limits}

% Which reference, and why this one?
As for the \gls{usv}, the second reference asks the \gls{uav} for motions it cannot perform, with each manoeuvre designed to exceed a different limit, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_limits_top}. The first arc, of $15\,\text{m}$ radius at $10.5\,\text{m/s}$, requires a tilt of about $37^\circ$, beyond the limit of $28.6^\circ$, and the second requires a horizontal speed of $11\,\text{m/s}$, beyond the limit of $10\,\text{m/s}$. After moving away from the \gls{usv} and down, the reference performs two steps in height, the first up to $10\,\text{m}$ and the second down to the surface of the water, below the minimum height of $2\,\text{m}$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics{Results/figures/01_tracking/limits_top.pdf}
    \caption{Top view of the reference beyond the limits of the \gls{uav} and path flown.}
    \label{fig:res_uav_limits_top}
\end{figure}

% What happens when the tilt or the horizontal speed cannot follow the reference?
On each arc, the \gls{nmpc} drives the \gls{uav} to the limits that make the reference unattainable and holds it there until the end of the arc, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_limits_horizontal}. On the first arc, the tilt reaches its limit at the maximum rate of the commanded roll and pitch, and the horizontal speed soon reaches its own limit as well. The tether, attached to the \gls{usv} at the centre of the arc, pulls the \gls{uav} inwards and adds to the horizontal force of the rotors. The centripetal acceleration therefore reaches about $7\,\text{m/s}^2$, against the $5.4\,\text{m/s}^2$ that the rotors provide with the tilt on its limit, so that the \gls{uav} holds $10\,\text{m/s}$ on this arc. On the second arc, the tilt reaches its limit only while the \gls{uav} accelerates, and the horizontal speed then settles on its limit with the tilt well below its own. In both cases the horizontal speed stays on its limit without oscillation, so that each arc is flown as fast as the limits allow.

% How far does the UAV fall behind the reference?
The \gls{uav} therefore falls behind the reference, by up to about $5\,\text{m}$ at the end of the second arc. Since the reference lies ahead along the arc, the \gls{nmpc} steers the \gls{uav} towards it across the inside of the arc, so that the \gls{uav} flies inside the reference, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_limits_top}. When the reference comes to rest, the \gls{uav} recovers this lag within about two seconds.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics{Results/figures/01_tracking/limits_horizontal.pdf}
    \caption{Tilt, its rate and horizontal speed of the \gls{uav} against their limits.}
    \label{fig:res_uav_limits_horizontal}
\end{figure}

% What happens on the steps in height?
On the steps in height, the \gls{uav} climbs and descends at the maximum vertical speed of $3\,\text{m/s}$, as shown in Figure~\ref{fig:res_uav_limits_vertical}, which it reaches through a rapid change of the thrust, with the thrust and its rate within their limits. During the descent, the minimum height enters the prediction horizon before the \gls{uav} reaches it, and the \gls{nmpc} begins to decelerate at about $3.3\,\text{m}$, so that the \gls{uav} comes to rest at $2\,\text{m}$ even though the reference lies at the surface of the water. Since stopping from $3\,\text{m/s}$ takes about $1.3\,\text{m}$, a controller that reacts to this limit only upon reaching it would cross it.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics{Results/figures/01_tracking/limits_vertical.pdf}
    \caption{Height, vertical speed, thrust and thrust rate of the \gls{uav} against their limits.}
    \label{fig:res_uav_limits_vertical}
\end{figure}

% Are the limits respected?
Throughout the test, the limits on the inputs, which are hard constraints, are reached but never exceeded. The limits on the states targeted by the reference are soft constraints, which the \gls{nmpc} may exceed when no feasible solution exists, and are exceeded by less than $1\,\%$ and only in brief transients. As for the \gls{usv}, the controller therefore gives up following the reference only as far as the limits of the \gls{uav} require, and uses its full capability in doing so.
