\chapter{Results}
\label{chapter:results}

% What remains to be shown, after everything built in the previous chapters?
The previous chapters developed models of the two vehicles and of the tether, and a controller for each vehicle that relies on them. Whether these controllers can operate the tethered system at sea can only be judged by operating it, where the limits of each vehicle, the coupling through the tether and the disturbances of the sea all act at once.

% How will it be shown?
Evaluated all together, these effects would not reveal which of them is responsible for a given behaviour. The evaluation therefore introduces them one at a time, moving gradually closer to a real operation. It begins with each vehicle alone, where only its own dynamics and limits are involved, then exposes the landing on the moving deck, the manoeuvre most sensitive to the sea, to waves and wind that the models do not describe, and finally couples the two vehicles through the tether in complete missions.
