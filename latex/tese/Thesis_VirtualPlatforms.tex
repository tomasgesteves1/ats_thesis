\chapter{Virtual Platforms and Simulation Setup}
\label{chapter:virtual_platforms}

This chapter describes the virtual hardware platforms and the simulation environment used to implement, test, and validate the cooperative control architecture. To bridge the gap between theoretical controller design and real-world deployment, the system is modeled and evaluated using high-fidelity simulations. The virtual setup comprises three primary components: the WAM-V surface vessel, the x500 quadrotor, and the physical coupling through a tether and active winch system. This chapter provides the physical characteristics and simulation dynamics of these platforms.

\section{WAM-V Autonomous Surface Vessel}
\label{sec:plat_usv}

The autonomous surface vessel utilized in this work is based on the \gls{usv} WAM-V (Wave Adaptive Modular Vessel) catamaran, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:coordinate_frames}. The WAM-V is a lightweight marine platform designed for coastal and offshore autonomy, characterized by two inflatable pontoons connected to a central structural payload tray by a suspension system. 

In the simulation environment, the WAM-V is represented as a rigid body with $6$ degrees of freedom (DOF). The physical characteristics of the simulated catamaran hull are summarized below:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Dimensions and Inertia:} The catamaran has an overall length of $4.0\,\text{m}$ and a beam width of $2.4\,\text{m}$. The nominal rigid-body mass of the empty vessel is $m = 259.10\,\text{kg}$, with a moment of inertia about the yaw axis of $I_{zz} = 527.70\,\text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$.
    \item \textbf{Propulsion Configuration:} The vessel is configured with a dual steerable stern-thruster system. The thrusters are mounted at a longitudinal arm of $x_{arm} = -2.37\,\text{m}$ (behind the center of gravity $\mathcal{O}_v$) and lateral arms of $y_{arm} = \pm 1.03\,\text{m}$ relative to the centerline. This allows for both differential thrust and vectoring of the propulsion force.
    \item \textbf{Thruster Asymmetry:} Each thruster exhibits asymmetrical thrust limits due to propeller blade design, yielding reduced efficiency when operating in reverse. Nominally, the reverse thrust is scaled by a factor of $0.746$ relative to the forward thrust capacity.
\end{itemize}

The simulation of the WAM-V in Gazebo Harmonic incorporates high-fidelity hydrostatic buoyancy and hydrodynamic drag. The buoyancy is computed dynamically by integrating Archimedes' principle over the discretized volumetric mesh of the pontoons. The hydrodynamics are simulated using Gazebo's lift-drag and fluid-damping plugins, which apply non-linear resistance forces and moments to replicate the hull's drag, cross-coupling, and wave-induced heave, roll, and pitch disturbances.

\section{x500 Unmanned Aerial Vehicle}
\label{sec:plat_uav}

The aerial platform is modeled after the Holybro x500 quadrotor development kit, which is a $500\,\text{mm}$-class quadrotor frame widely used for research and development. 

The simulated UAV has the following physical characteristics:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mass Properties:} The total mass of the quadrotor frame is approximately $2.06\,\text{kg}$, consisting of a $2.00\,\text{kg}$ main central body link and four rotors weighing $0.016\,\text{kg}$ each.
    \item \textbf{Actuation:} The quadrotor is propelled by four brushless DC motors equipped with $10$-inch propellers, arranged in a standard X-configuration. The rotor response curves, blade aerodynamics, and motor speed lag are modeled in the simulator to capture the transient dynamics of thrust production.
    \item \textbf{Autopilot Response Characteristic:} The vehicle exhibits a characteristic first-order response lag in attitude tracking, which represents the dynamic behavior of the inner-loop cascaded rate and attitude controllers of the flight control unit.
\end{itemize}

\section{Tether and Winch Coupling System}
\label{sec:plat_tether}

The physical coupling between the WAM-V and the x500 quadrotor is established by a flexible tether cable managed by an active winch system mounted on the catamaran's deck.

The tether is simulated using two distinct representations depending on the test requirements:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Multibody Joint Chain (Gazebo Physics):} For visual feedback and rigid-body contact handling (such as collisions with the catamaran deck or water surface), the tether is modeled as a chain of $30$ rigid cylindrical links connected by spherical (ball) joints. Each link has a radius of $0.01\,\text{m}$ (1 cm) and a mass of $0.02\,\text{kg}$, yielding a total tether mass of $0.6\,\text{kg}$ for a nominal $5.0\,\text{m}$ length. Joint damping of $2.0\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s}/\text{m}$ and friction of $0.1\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}$ are applied at each joint to model internal bending resistance.
    \item \textbf{MoorDyn Lumped-Mass Physics:} For high-fidelity cable dynamics and tension propagation, the MoorDyn physics library is integrated. MoorDyn discretizes the cable into lumped mass nodes connected by spring-damper segments. It computes longitudinal stiffness, internal structural damping, gravity, buoyancy, and transverse/longitudinal hydrodynamic drag forces acting on the line.
\end{enumerate}

The winch is located on the WAM-V deck at a fixed offset of $\mathbf{p}_{a/v} = [-0.8, 0.0, 0.0]^T\,\text{m}$ relative to the vessel's center of gravity $\mathcal{O}_v$. The winch mechanism controls the unstretched length of the tether ($L_{tether}$) through active spooling, supporting geometric mode spooling (tracking the distance between vehicles) and tension-based spooling (maintaining a target line tension).

\section{Simulation Setup and Environmental Physics}
\label{sec:sim_environment}

The virtual platforms are integrated and simulated within the Gazebo Harmonic environment. The simulation framework is configured to resolve the multi-body physics and emulate realistic maritime environmental conditions, providing a physical testbed for the control strategies:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physics Solver Configuration:} The simulation utilizes the DART (Dynamic Animation and Robotics Toolkit) physics engine, configured with a step size of $0.004\,\text{s}$ ($250\,\text{Hz}$). This rate balances calculation accuracy for the stiff multi-body dynamics of the tether chain against real-time simulation performance.
    \item \textbf{Maritime Environmental Disturbances:} To replicate realistic operation at sea, the Gazebo environment incorporates physical wave generators and current fields. The water surface is modeled with non-linear wave profiles (using a Gerstner wave representation) that apply dynamic heave, roll, and pitch forces directly to the pontoons of the WAM-V.
    \item \textbf{Wind and Aerodynamic Drag:} The aerial workspace includes wind velocity fields that exert aerodynamic drag forces on the x500 quadrotor's body and rotors, acting as a continuous, time-varying disturbance during flight.
    \item \textbf{Collision and Contact Resolution:} Full rigid-body contact mechanics are resolved between all physical links in the simulation, including the collision geometry of the catamaran deck, the tether's cylindrical links, and the quadrotor's landing gear.
\end{itemize}
