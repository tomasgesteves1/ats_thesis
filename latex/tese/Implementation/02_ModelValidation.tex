\section{Models Validation}
\label{sec:model_validation}

Before incorporating the vehicle and tether models into the real-time controllers developed in Chapter~\ref{chapter:methodology}, their accuracy must be verified against the high-fidelity simulation environment described in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_environment} to ensure they capture the dominant dynamics while remaining computationally tractable. Each model is evaluated using the nominal simulator parameters, without fitting them to the validation data, driven by recorded inputs from representative manoeuvres. Model fidelity is quantified by the \gls{rmse} in physical units and by the \gls{nrmse}, defined as the \gls{rmse} normalised by the signal range.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\gls{usv} Model}{USV Model}}
\label{subsec:usv_validation}

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{\gls{uav} Model}{UAV Model}}
\label{subsec:uav_validation}

The second model to be validated is the one used by the \gls{uav} \gls{nmpc}, presented in Section~\ref{sec:ctrl_uav_mpc}. It predicts how the vehicle moves from the attitude and the thrust that are commanded, so its accuracy determines how well the controller can anticipate that motion. The tether force is left out of the model, so that the motion of the vehicle is validated on its own, and the tether is validated separately in Section~\ref{subsec:tether_model_validation}. The parameters of the model are obtained from the simulated vehicle, described in Section~\ref{sec:x500_quadrotor}, and are listed in Table~\ref{tab:uav_model_parameters}.

\begin{table}[!htbp]
    \centering
    \caption{Parameters of the \gls{uav} model.}
    \label{tab:uav_model_parameters}
    \begin{tabular}{llr}
        \toprule
        \textbf{Parameter} & \textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Value} \\
        \midrule
        Mass & $m$ & $2.06\,\text{kg}$ \\
        Gravitational acceleration & $g$ & $9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ \\
        Rotor drag coefficient & $d_r$ & $0.218\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s}/\text{m}$ \\
        Attitude time constant (roll and pitch) & $\tau$ & $0.154\,\text{s}$ \\
        Attitude time constant (yaw) & $\tau_\psi$ & $0.357\,\text{s}$ \\
        Transport delay (roll and pitch) & $\tau_d$ & $0.04\,\text{s}$ \\
        Transport delay (yaw) & $\tau_{d,\psi}$ & $0.06\,\text{s}$ \\
        \bottomrule
    \end{tabular}
\end{table}

The mass and the gravitational acceleration are taken directly from the simulator, but the other parameters need to be calculated. The simulator applies to each rotor a drag force proportional to both its angular speed and the velocity of the vehicle, through the coefficient $c_{drag} = 8.06\times10^{-5}$ of Table~\ref{tab:x500_parameters}. Around the hovering speed $\omega_0$, at which the thrust of the four rotors balances the weight, the four rotors together give a linear drag on the horizontal velocity,
\begin{equation}
    \omega_0 = \sqrt{\frac{mg}{4\,k_F}} \approx 675\,\text{rad/s}, \qquad d_r = 4\,c_{drag}\,\omega_0 \approx 0.218\,\text{N}\cdot\text{s}/\text{m},
    \label{eq:uav_drag_derivation}
\end{equation}
where $k_F$ is the thrust coefficient of the rotors. The nominal attitude time constants are not parameters of the simulator, since they result from the attitude controller of the vehicle. This controller converts the angle error into an angular rate setpoint through a proportional gain, $P_\phi = 6.5\,\text{s}^{-1}$ for roll and pitch and $P_\psi = 2.8\,\text{s}^{-1}$ for yaw. Assuming that the inner loop that tracks the angular rate does so instantly, the angular rate of the vehicle is equal to the setpoint, and the roll angle $\phi$ evolves as
\begin{equation}
    \dot\phi = P_\phi\,(\phi_{\mathrm{cmd}} - \phi),
    \label{eq:uav_attitude_loop}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{\mathrm{cmd}}$ is the commanded roll angle, and the same relation holds for the pitch and, with the gain $P_\psi$, for the yaw. This is a first-order response, in which the attitude changes faster the further it is from the command, and its time constant is the inverse of the gain,
\begin{equation}
    \tau = \frac{1}{P_\phi} \approx 0.154\,\text{s}, \qquad \tau_\psi = \frac{1}{P_\psi} \approx 0.357\,\text{s}.
    \label{eq:uav_tau_nominal}
\end{equation}
The attitude controller does not, however, act on the roll and pitch angles separately. It rotates the vehicle so that the direction of the thrust approaches the commanded direction, and it controls the yaw independently, so the time constant $\tau$ applies to the direction of the thrust, as in the model of Section~\ref{sec:ctrl_uav_mpc}. The commands also reach the attitude controller only after a small transport delay. The delays are not parameters of the simulator either, and they were obtained from flights with the \gls{nmpc}, different from the validation flight, as the values that minimise the error of the attitude predicted over $1\,\text{s}$, giving $\tau_d = 0.04\,\text{s}$ for roll and pitch and $\tau_{d,\psi} = 0.06\,\text{s}$ for yaw.

\subsection{Tether Model}
\label{subsec:tether_model_validation}

% o que é validado e com que parâmetros
The last model to be validated is the tether force model of Section~\ref{subsec:mod_tether_force_model}, which gives the force that the tether applies on the \gls{uav} from the positions of the two vehicles. The force has a tension, proportional to the distance $d$ between the vehicles, and a direction. The chord, the straight line between the vehicles, would be the direction of the force if the tether were weightless, but the weight of the tether bends it and rotates the force from the chord towards the vertical. The tension is approximately the weight of the tether hanging between the two vehicles, $T_{\mathrm{tether}} = \mu g L$, where $\mu$ is the linear mass and $L$ the length of the tether. Since the winch keeps $L = d/(1-\varepsilon_0)$, the tension is proportional to the distance between the vehicles, $T_{\mathrm{tether}} = c_d\, d$, with
\begin{equation}
    c_d = \frac{\mu g}{1-\varepsilon_0} = \frac{0.020 \times 9.81}{1-0.05} = 0.2065\,\text{N/m}.
\end{equation}
The deflection of the force is largest when the chord is horizontal and decreases as it approaches the vertical. For a horizontal chord, the weight bends the tether into a catenary~\cite{irvine1981cable}, and the deflection $\gamma_0$ is the angle that the tether makes with the chord at the \gls{uav}. For a tether of length $L$ between two points at distance $d$, it is given by
\begin{equation}
    \gamma_0 = \arctan\left(\sinh \kappa\right), \qquad \frac{\sinh \kappa}{\kappa} = \frac{L}{d} = \frac{1}{1-\varepsilon_0},
\end{equation}
where $\kappa$ is the positive solution of the second equation, obtained iteratively. For $\varepsilon_0 = 5\%$, this gives $\kappa = 0.558$ and $\gamma_0 = 30.4^\circ$. The parameters of the model are listed in Table~\ref{tab:tether_model_parameters}.

% como é feita a comparação
The tether force was recorded in the simulator during a flight of $60.5\,\text{s}$, in which the \gls{uav} moved along $x$, and the distance between the two vehicles varied between $4.6\,\text{m}$ and $14.0\,\text{m}$. The winch regulated the slack to $\varepsilon_0$. The reference is the force computed by MoorDyn and applied on the \gls{uav} in Gazebo described in Section~\ref{sec:simulation_environment}. At each instant, the model receives the positions of the two vehicles and computes the force, which is compared with the reference in tension and in its components in the inertial frame. The comparison is also made for two simpler models of the force, which differ from the catenary model only in its direction: the taut model, in which the tether is taut and the force points along the chord, and the vertical model, in which the tether hangs from the \gls{uav} as a vertical load. Since the models give the force directly from the positions, there is no initial state and no error accumulates, so the whole flight is compared instant by instant.

% quão perto está o modelo do simulador
Figure~\ref{fig:model_fit_comparison} compares the tension of the model with that of the simulator along the flight, and Figure~\ref{fig:model_fit_3d} compares the components of the force on the \gls{uav} along $x$ and $z$. The catenary model follows the tension and the components, with errors between $4\%$ and $11\%$ of their range, as listed in Table~\ref{tab:tether_model_benchmark}. The two simpler models fail in opposite ways. The vertical model gives no force along $x$ and places the whole tension along $z$, while the taut model, which keeps the force along the chord, overestimates it along $x$ and underestimates it along $z$.

Since the three models use the same tension, their errors differ only through the direction of the force. The catenary model reduces the error of the force by $72\%$ relative to the taut model and by $76\%$ relative to the vertical model, and its error of $0.25\,\text{N}$ represents $1.2\%$ of the weight of the \gls{uav}, which is $20.2\,\text{N}$.
