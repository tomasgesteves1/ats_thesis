\section{Manoeuvres and Missions}
\label{sec:maneuvers_and_missions}

% What produces the references that the controllers follow?
The controllers of Section~\ref{sec:controller_implementation} follow the references they are given, but do not decide where each vehicle should go. This is the role of the manoeuvres, which turn a simple motion, such as an orbit around the \gls{usv} or a landing on its deck, into the reference of each controller at every instant. The missions then chain these manoeuvres, for both vehicles, into a complete operation.

\subsection{Manoeuvres}
\label{subsec:maneuver_primitives}

% What is a manoeuvre?
A manoeuvre gives the reference of one vehicle as a function of time, sampled over the horizon of the controller at every instant. Five manoeuvres were defined for the \gls{uav}, takeoff, formation, orbit, follow and landing, and four for the \gls{usv}, which holds its position, sails a route of waypoints, follows a target, keeping a safe distance from it, or supports the \gls{uav}. Each is set by a few parameters, such as a destination or a speed. The references are simple geometric curves in time, such as lines and circles, since the controllers keep the vehicles within their limits whatever reference they are given, even a single point.

% Relative to what are the references given?
Most manoeuvres of the \gls{uav} are given relative to the winch, since the vehicle works around the \gls{usv} and must return to it. Their references move with the \gls{usv} along the trajectory predicted by its controller, either in fixed axes or in the axes of the \gls{usv}, so that they turn with it, as when the \gls{uav} keeps ahead of the bow. The height is taken above $z_t$, the constant height of the winch in calm water, so that the \gls{uav} does not follow the heave of the deck. The point of the formation and the centre of the orbit can also be fixed in the world, to reach or circle a structure, and the reference of the follow manoeuvre moves with the target.

% How does the USV support the UAV?
When the \gls{uav} circles a structure, a tether anchored at a fixed point would wrap around it. The \gls{usv} then supports the \gls{uav}, taking as its reference the point on the water below the \gls{uav}, pushed away from the structure to a safe distance, and sailing along with it as it moves around the structure. This is only possible when the reference of the \gls{uav} is fixed in the world, since a reference given relative to the winch would move with the \gls{usv}, and each vehicle would then chase the other.

% Where does the UAV look?
Each manoeuvre also sets the heading of the \gls{uav}, through the point that its controller points it at, described in Section~\ref{sec:ctrl_uav_mpc}, such as the winch, a structure or a target, so that a camera on the vehicle keeps it in view. To hold a given heading instead, this point is placed ahead of the winch along that heading, which for the takeoff and the landing is the heading of the \gls{usv}, so that the vehicle is aligned with the deck.

% What do the manoeuvres look like in practice?
Figure~\ref{fig:maneuver_catalogue} shows the manoeuvres of the \gls{uav} as performed in simulation, seen from above in fixed axes, with the two vehicles drawn at the same instants, the older ones more transparent, so that their motion can be seen together. The formation (b) keeps the \gls{uav} ahead of the bow, in the axes of the \gls{usv}, while it follows a route, and the orbit (c) circles the \gls{usv} as it sails, shown in its axes, where the orbit is a circle, while in (d) the \gls{uav} orbits the \gls{usv} on patrol until it detects a target, which it then follows (e) together with the \gls{usv}. The takeoff and the landing (a, f) are shown instead through the height of the \gls{uav} above $z_t$, which shows the heave of the deck.

% How does the UAV take off from the moving deck?
The takeoff and the landing are the manoeuvres in which the \gls{uav} is closest to the deck and moves with it. Both start or end on a landing pad of $0.40\,\text{m}$ by $0.40\,\text{m}$ at the centre of the deck, bordered by inclined walls and placed directly above the winch. To hold the vehicle against the deck, so that the waves do not tip it over and throw it into the sea, the winch pulls the tether, as described in Section~\ref{sec:tether_winch}, from the start of the takeoff until the \gls{uav} is clear of the deck. The controller of the \gls{uav} does not include this pull in its model, and the vertical component of its disturbance estimate is held constant while the pull acts, so that the estimate does not cancel the pull, which keeps acting on the \gls{uav} and holds the tether taut. The takeoff therefore begins with a vertical climb off the pad, phase~1 in Figure~\ref{fig:maneuver_catalogue}a, and only then moves the vehicle to its first position, phase~2, so that it does not drift over the edge of the deck at low height.

% How does the UAV land on the moving deck?
The landing is divided into four phases, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:maneuver_catalogue}f and Figure~\ref{fig:landing_phases}. The \gls{uav} first moves above the winch (1) and waits until it is centred above the pad and moving with it (2). If it is not centred within $40\,\text{s}$, the landing on the moving deck is aborted and the \gls{usv} starts the recovery of the \gls{uav}, described in Section~\ref{subsec:missions_coordination}. It then descends with the winch pulling it towards the deck (3), and ends with a slow final approach towards a point below the deck (4), so that it touches down even if the deck falls away, and the speed of the contact is set by the heave rather than by the descent. Once it rests on the pad, the vehicle is disarmed, and the winch keeps it against the deck.

\subsection{Missions}
\label{subsec:missions_coordination}

% What is a mission?
A mission is a file with two sequences of manoeuvres, one for each vehicle. Each manoeuvre ends when a condition is met, either an event, such as the detection of a target, or the end of a manoeuvre of the other vehicle.

% What happens when something goes wrong?
A mission is aborted when a manoeuvre fails, when it takes too long, or when the operator requests it. The \gls{usv} then holds its position and the \gls{uav} lands.

% Which missions were tested?
Three missions were tested in simulation, shown in Figure~\ref{fig:missions}. In the inspection (a), the \gls{uav} circles a tower standing in the water, $43\,\text{m}$ high, at three heights while the \gls{usv} supports it. In the patrol (b), the \gls{uav} orbits the \gls{usv} until it detects a moving target, which both vehicles then follow. The target is a second \gls{wamv} that patrols a forbidden zone at $0.6\,\text{m/s}$ and flees at $2\,\text{m/s}$ when either vehicle comes within $15\,\text{m}$, and it is detected when it enters the view of a camera on the \gls{uav}, pointed downwards and assumed to cover $120^\circ$. In the recovery (c), the \gls{uav} is held at a point it cannot leave, while the \gls{usv} sails away, waits at the reach of the tether and returns to collect it.
