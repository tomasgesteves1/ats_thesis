\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl_future}

% What is the next step for the system as a whole?
The system was evaluated in simulation only, with the state of both vehicles taken from the simulator. The next step is to test it with the real vehicles, where the state is estimated from noisy sensors, the communication between the vehicles has delays, and the tether is stiffer than the one simulated, so that its tension changes faster with the distance. The models would first need to be validated again against the real vehicles, and their parameters identified wherever the nominal values do not hold.

% How could the takeoff and the landing be improved?
The takeoff and the landing are the manoeuvres in which the \gls{uav} is closest to the moving deck. In the landing, the speed of the contact grows with the waves, and a prediction of the motion of the deck could time the touchdown to when the deck is moving down. The takeoff was not tested under wind, which could tip the \gls{uav} against the pad while the winch still holds it, and starting the estimate of the disturbance observer before the \gls{uav} leaves the deck, or turning the \gls{usv} so that the \gls{uav} faces the wind, could make it more robust.

% How could the missions become more flexible?
The missions are fixed sequences of manoeuvres, in which every manoeuvre ends with an event or with the end of a manoeuvre of the other vehicle. Real operations also need missions that branch, for instance to return to the search when the target is lost, or to change the route when the sea is too rough to land. A reactive mission layer, such as a behaviour tree, could choose the next manoeuvre from the state of both vehicles, and a higher-level interface could let an operator describe the mission in more general terms, leaving its decomposition into manoeuvres to the system.

% How could the vehicles operate around obstacles?
Since the controllers plan the motion within all their limits from simple references, the manoeuvres could be replaced by references planned over a longer horizon, for instance around obstacles, without changing the controllers. Obstacles described by simple shapes could also be added to both \glspl{nmpc} as further constraints on the vehicles and on points along the tether, which would let the two vehicles operate close to structures without a dedicated manoeuvre to keep the tether clear of them.
