\chapter{System Modeling and Validation}
\label{chapter:modeling}

This chapter presents the modeling and validation of the cooperative \gls{uav}-\gls{usv} system. The modeling is divided into three main components: the WAM-V catamaran, the x500 quadrotor, and the tether and winch system. Each component is modeled with a focus on control-oriented dynamics suitable for real-time execution of the \gls{nmpc} algorithm. The models are validated against high-fidelity simulations and experimental data to ensure accuracy and reliability.

\section{Reference Coordinate Frames}
\label{sec:mod_coordinates}

To describe the motion and coupling of the cooperative \gls{uav}-\gls{usv} system, it is necessary to establish the reference coordinate frames. The transformations between these frames must also be defined. In this work, three primary coordinate systems are defined, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:coordinate_frames}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Inertial Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$}: A local flat-Earth coordinate system defined by the origin $\mathcal{O}_I$ and axes $\{\mathbf{x}_I, \mathbf{y}_I, \mathbf{z}_I\}$. This frame is oriented according to the \gls{enu} convention. The axis $\mathbf{x}_I$ points East. The axis $\mathbf{y}_I$ points North. The axis $\mathbf{z}_I$ points vertically upward, parallel to the gravity vector.
    \item \textbf{\Gls{usv} Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$}: Centered at the WAM-V catamaran's \gls{cg} $\mathcal{O}_v$ (where the subscript $v$ denotes the surface vessel, or \gls{usv}) with axes $\{\mathbf{x}_v, \mathbf{y}_v, \mathbf{z}_v\}$. The longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_v$ points forward, representing the surge direction. The lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_v$ points to port or left, representing the sway direction. The vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_v$ points upward, representing the heave direction.
    \item \textbf{\Gls{uav} Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$}: Centered at the x500 quadrotor's \gls{cg} $\mathcal{O}_a$ (where the subscript $a$ denotes the aerial vehicle, or \gls{uav}) with axes $\{\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{y}_a, \mathbf{z}_a\}$. The axis $\mathbf{x}_a$ points forward. The axis $\mathbf{y}_a$ points to the left. The axis $\mathbf{z}_a$ points upward.
\end{itemize}

The position vector of the \gls{usv} \gls{cg} in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is defined as $\mathbf{p}_v = [x_v, y_v, z_v]^T$. Similarly, the position vector of the \gls{uav} \gls{cg} in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{p}_a = [x_a, y_a, z_a]^T$. Under nominal operations, the \gls{usv} moves strictly on the water surface. Thus, $z_v = 0$ is assumed.

The physical tether anchor on the WAM-V deck is located at a fixed offset vector $\mathbf{p}_{a/v} = [x_{a/v}, y_{a/v}, z_{a/v}]^T$ relative to the \gls{usv} body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$. The global position of this anchor point in the inertial frame is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{anchor} = \mathbf{p}_v + \mathbf{R}_v(\psi) \mathbf{p}_{a/v}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}_v(\psi)$ is the rotation matrix in the Special Orthogonal group $SO(3)$ representing the orientation of the \gls{usv}.

The \gls{usv} roll and pitch motions are neglected in the control-oriented model. Thus, the rotation from $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ to $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is parameterized only by the yaw heading angle $\psi$. This rotation represents a planar movement about the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_I$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_rot_matrix}
    \mathbf{R}_v(\psi) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\
        \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

The orientation of the \gls{uav} body frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ relative to the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is parameterized by three Euler angles. These are the roll $\phi$, pitch $\theta$, and yaw $\psi_a$ angles. The rotation follows the Z-Y-X sequence representing yaw, pitch, and roll, respectively. The resulting rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ in the Special Orthogonal group $SO(3)$ is:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uav_rot_matrix}
    \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_a \cos\theta & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi - \sin\psi_a \cos\phi & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        \sin\psi_a \cos\theta & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi + \cos\psi_a \cos\phi & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \sin\phi & \cos\theta \cos\phi
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

This kinematic mapping relates the body-fixed coordinates to the inertial space. It is required to enforce tether constraints and coordinate the trajectory tracking of both vehicles.


\section{WAM-V Surface Vessel Modeling and Validation}
\label{sec:mod_usv}

This section describes the kinematics and dynamics modeling of the WAM-V (Wave Adaptive Modular Vessel) catamaran. The platform serves as the autonomous surface vessel. The modeling progresses from planar \gls{usv} kinematics to a simplified 3-\gls{dof} control-oriented horizontal Fossen model. Finally, the dynamic parameters are calibrated and validated using telemetry.

\subsection{USV Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_kinematics}

The \gls{usv} operates on the 2D water surface. The position vector of the \gls{usv} \gls{cg} in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{\eta}_v = [x_v, y_v, \psi]^T$. The velocity vector in the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ is defined as $\mathbf{\nu}_v = [u, v, r]^T$. Here, $u$ is the surge velocity, $v$ is the sway velocity, and $r$ is the yaw angular velocity. The kinematic relationship mapping body-fixed velocities to the inertial frame is given by:
\begin{align}
    \dot{x}_v &= u \cos\psi - v \sin\psi \\
    \dot{y}_v &= u \sin\psi + v \cos\psi \\
    \dot{\psi} &= r
\end{align}

This relationship assumes that heave ($z$), roll ($\phi$), and pitch ($\theta$) displacements are zero. This simplification is adopted for planar path following. These degrees of freedom remain active in the simulation environment.

\subsection{Control-Oriented Dynamic Model (Fossen 3-DOF)}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_control_model}

For the real-time execution of the \gls{nmpc} algorithm, a simplified horizontal 3-\gls{dof} model is adopted. Following Fossen's notation, the equations of motion are formulated in the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ as:
\begin{align}
    \dot{u} &= \frac{X - d_u(u) u}{m} + v r \label{eq:usv_surge_dot} \\
    \dot{v} &= \frac{Y - d_v(v) v}{m} - u r \label{eq:usv_sway_dot} \\
    \dot{r} &= \frac{N - d_r(r) r}{I_{zz}} \label{eq:usv_yaw_dot}
\end{align}
where $m$ is the rigid-body mass, $I_{zz}$ is the moment of inertia about the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_v$, and the inputs $X, Y, N$ represent the virtual surge force, sway force, and yaw moment applied at the \gls{cg}, respectively. The terms $+vr$ and $-ur$ represent the centrifugal and Coriolis accelerations resulting from the rotating body frame.

The non-linear damping forces $d_u(u)$, $d_v(v)$, and $d_r(r)$ account for drag. To capture the longitudinal asymmetry of the catamaran hull, the surge damping has directional dependence. This represents the differences in drag between the bow and the stern:
\begin{align}
    d_u(u) &= \begin{cases} 
        X_U + X_{UU} |u|, & \text{if } u \ge 0 \quad \text{(forward motion)} \\
        X_U + X_{UU\_BWD} |u|, & \text{if } u < 0 \quad \text{(backward motion)}
    \end{cases} \\
    d_v(v) &= Y_V + Y_{VV} |v| \\
    d_r(r) &= N_R + N_{RR} |r|
\end{align}
where $X_U, Y_V, N_R$ are linear damping coefficients, and $X_{UU}, X_{UU\_BWD}, Y_{VV}, N_{RR}$ are quadratic damping coefficients.

\subsection{Thruster Allocation Mapping}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_propulsion}

The WAM-V is equipped with two steerable stern thrusters. These are located at a longitudinal arm $x_{arm}$ behind the \gls{cg}. They are placed at lateral arms $\pm y_{arm}$ relative to the centerline. Let $T_L$ and $T_R$ represent the thrust magnitudes. Let $\alpha_L$ and $\alpha_R$ represent the steering angles of the left and right thrusters, respectively.

The thrusters exhibit reduced propulsion efficiency in reverse. This is due to the propeller blade geometry. The asymmetry is incorporated by defining an effective thrust $T_{i, eff}$ for each motor $i \in \{L, R\}$ as:
\begin{equation}
    T_{i, eff} = \begin{cases}
        T_i, & \text{if } T_i \ge 0 \\
        0.746 \cdot T_i, & \text{if } T_i < 0
    \end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $0.746$ is the reverse thrust efficiency multiplier. 

The virtual forces and moment $[X, Y, N]^T$ applied at the \gls{cg} are mapped from the physical thruster inputs by the thruster allocation equations:
\begin{align}
    X &= T_{L, eff} \cos\alpha_L + T_{R, eff} \cos\alpha_R \\
    Y &= T_{L, eff} \sin\alpha_L + T_{R, eff} \sin\alpha_R \\
    N &= x_{arm} \left( T_{L, eff} \sin\alpha_L + T_{R, eff} \sin\alpha_R \right) - y_{arm} \left( T_{L, eff} \cos\alpha_L - T_{R, eff} \cos\alpha_R \right)
\end{align}

This allocation mapping is implemented inside the C++ control pipeline. It maps the virtual forces of the \gls{nmpc} down to the physical thrusters.

\subsection{Dynamic Model Validation and Calibration}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_validation}

To calibrate and validate the horizontal 3-DOF dynamic model of the WAM-V, a systematic two-step identification process is conducted. First, a set of dynamic excitation maneuvers is performed to collect estimation data. Second, the parameters are optimized by minimizing the difference between simulated and simulated-ground-truth states. Finally, the model is validated using a separate, independent excitation dataset to ensure generalization.

The parameter calibration relies on distinct excitation signals. The excitation maneuvers are designed to decouple the vehicle dynamics across all active degrees of freedom. Figure~\ref{fig:usv_excitation_signals} shows the multi-phased excitation signals used for model validation. The estimation phase and validation phase use different signal profiles to prevent overfitting. The excitation profile consists of four primary stages: Phase 1 introduces pure surge steps to identify longitudinal damping, Phase 2 applies differential thrust commands to excite yaw rate, Phase 3 forces lateral motion to isolate sway damping, and Phase 4 provides fully coupled random actuation to capture cross-coupling dynamics.

The dynamic parameters are calibrated using a grey-box estimation approach. The optimization process fits the Fossen 3-DOF model equations to the collected Gazebo simulator telemetry. This is achieved by solving a non-linear least-squares optimization problem. The cost function minimizes the tracking error of the linear and angular velocities:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\mathbf{\theta}} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \|\mathbf{\nu}_{v, actual}(k) - \mathbf{\nu}_{v, pred}(k, \mathbf{\theta})\|_2^2
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{\theta}$ is the vector of unknown hydrodynamic parameters. The predicted velocity vector $\mathbf{\nu}_{v, pred}$ is computed by integrating the dynamics using a fourth-order Runge-Kutta solver.

where $x_{i, actual}$ and $x_{i, pred}$ are the actual and predicted states, respectively, and $\bar{x}_{i, actual}$ is the mean of the actual state.

\section{x500 Quadrotor Modeling and Validation}
\label{sec:mod_uav}

\subsection{UAV Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_kinematics}

The \gls{uav} operates in 3D airspace. The position vector of the \gls{uav} \gls{cg} in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{p}_a = [x_a, y_a, z_a]^T$. The translational velocity vector of the vehicle, also expressed in the inertial reference frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$, is defined as $\mathbf{v}_a = [v_{x,a}, v_{y,a}, v_{z,a}]^T$. The kinematic relationship mapping the rate of change of the global position coordinates to the inertial velocity vector is directly given by:
\begin{align}
    \dot{x}_a &= v_{x,a} \\
    \dot{y}_a &= v_{y,a} \\
    \dot{z}_a &= v_{z,a}
\end{align}

Unlike the \gls{usv}, whose velocity coordinates are defined in the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ to simplify hydrodynamic force mapping, the translational velocities of the quadrotor are formulated in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$. This representation aligns with the design of the control-oriented model and avoids unnecessary coordinate rotations in the translational dynamics. The orientation of the quadrotor body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ relative to the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is defined by the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ given in \eqref{eq:uav_rot_matrix}.


\subsection{Control-Oriented Model (Attitude Lag)}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_control_model}

To achieve a control-oriented model suitable for real-time execution in the \gls{nmpc} framework, the full $6$-DOF rigid-body dynamics of the quadrotor are simplified. Instead of modeling the high-frequency rotational dynamics, rotor speeds, and torque mixing, a cascaded control assumption is adopted. The high-rate attitude inner loop of the PX4 autopilot is assumed to track commanded roll and pitch references with a characteristic first-order response lag.

The actual roll $\phi$ and pitch $\theta$ dynamics are therefore modeled as:
\begin{align}
    \dot{\phi} &= \frac{\phi_{cmd} - \phi}{\tau} \label{eq:uav_roll_dot} \\
    \dot{\theta} &= \frac{\theta_{cmd} - \theta}{\tau} \label{eq:uav_pitch_dot}
\end{align}
where $\tau$ is the inner-loop attitude response time constant, and $\phi_{cmd}$ and $\theta_{cmd}$ represent the commanded roll and pitch angle setpoints, respectively.

To prevent aggressive variations in the commanded setpoints and ensure smooth trajectory planning, the state vector is augmented to include the commanded attitude variables. By defining the time derivatives of these commands as the virtual control inputs, we have:
\begin{align}
    \dot{\phi}_{cmd} &= \dot{\phi}_{cmd, ctrl} \label{eq:uav_roll_cmd_dot} \\
    \dot{\theta}_{cmd} &= \dot{\theta}_{cmd, ctrl} \label{eq:uav_pitch_cmd_dot}
\end{align}
where $\dot{\phi}_{cmd, ctrl}$ and $\dot{\theta}_{cmd, ctrl}$ are the control variables computed by the NMPC. This formulation allows the optimizer to directly penalize the rate of command changes in the cost function, enforcing actuator smoothness and preventing abrupt drone maneuvers that could destabilize the coupled tethered system.

\subsection{Thrust Vectoring and Acceleration Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_acceleration}

The translational motion of the \gls{uav} is driven by the collective thrust generated by the four rotors, which acts along the body-fixed vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$. The thrust force per unit mass (mass-normalized thrust acceleration) is denoted by $a_T$. Using the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ from the body frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ to the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ defined in \eqref{eq:uav_rot_matrix}, the thrust acceleration vector in the global \gls{enu} coordinate system is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}_{thrust} = \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ a_T \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
By computing this product using the third column of the rotation matrix, the components of $\mathbf{a}_{thrust} = [a_{x,thrust}, a_{y,thrust}, a_{z,thrust}]^T$ are:
\begin{align}
    a_{x,thrust} &= a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \\
    a_{y,thrust} &= a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \\
    a_{z,thrust} &= a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi
\end{align}
where $\psi_a$ is the yaw heading angle of the drone, which is treated as a slowly varying external parameter in the control horizon.

Combining the thrust acceleration with the gravity vector $\mathbf{g} = [0, 0, -g]^T$, where $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is the acceleration due to gravity, the complete translational dynamics of the quadrotor are formulated in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ as:
\begin{align}
    \dot{v}_{x,a} &= a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vx_dot} \\
    \dot{v}_{y,a} &= a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vy_dot} \\
    \dot{v}_{z,a} &= a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi - g \label{eq:uav_vz_dot}
\end{align}

This formulation couples the attitude states $(\phi, \theta)$ directly to the translational acceleration of the vehicle, allowing the \gls{nmpc} to manipulate the quadrotor's trajectory in the 3D space by adjusting the attitude commands and thrust magnitude.

\subsection{Autopilot Inner-Loop System Identification}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_validation}

To identify and validate the inner-loop attitude response time constant $\tau$, a grey-box system identification process is performed. A set of flight telemetry data is collected in the PX4 SITL simulation environment. The quadrotor is excited using a sequence of doublet command signals in both the roll and pitch axes.

The time constant $\tau$ is estimated by solving a non-linear least-squares optimization problem that minimizes the discrepancy between the measured flight angles and the simulated output of the first-order response model. The optimization cost function is formulated as:
\begin{equation}
    \min_{\tau} \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left( \phi_{actual}(k) - \phi_{pred}(k, \tau) \right)^2
\end{equation}
where $\phi_{actual}$ is the roll angle telemetry from the autopilot, and $\phi_{pred}$ is the predicted angle computed by integrating the model equations using the commanded roll setpoints $\phi_{cmd}$ and a forward Euler integration scheme. A similar formulation is applied to the pitch axis.

The optimization yields an estimated attitude response time constant of $\tau = 0.15\,\text{s}$ (equivalent to a closed-loop attitude bandwidth of $1/\tau \approx 6.67\,\text{rad/s}$). Figure~\ref{fig:uav_sysid_validation} shows the comparison between the commanded roll angle, the telemetry recorded from PX4 SITL, and the simulated response of the identified first-order model. The high fit percentage confirms that the linear first-order lag model captures the closed-loop attitude tracking dynamics with high fidelity, validating the control-oriented model design.

\section{Tether and Winch Modeling and Validation}
\label{sec:mod_tether_winch}


\subsection{Control-Oriented Geometric Constraints}
\label{subsec:mod_geometric_tether}

For the formulation of the real-time \gls{nmpc} algorithm, modeling the full multi-body or lumped-mass catenary dynamics of the tether inside the optimization prediction horizon is computationally prohibitive. Instead, the coupling effect is modeled as a geometric boundary constraint. The tether is assumed to act as a spherical workspace envelope of radius $L_{tether}$ centered at the USV anchor point $\mathbf{p}_{anchor} = [x_{boat}, y_{boat}, z_{boat}]^T$. 

Assuming the USV moves on the water surface ($z_{boat} = 0$), the geometric distance $d_{tether}$ between the \gls{uav} position $\mathbf{p}_a = [x_a, y_a, z_a]^T$ and the shipboard anchor is expressed as:
\begin{equation}
    d_{tether} = \sqrt{(x_a - x_{boat})^2 + (y_a - y_{boat})^2 + z_a^2}
\end{equation}

To enforce that the UAV remains within the physical boundary of the tether, a non-linear path constraint is formulated in the optimal control problem:
\begin{equation}
    0 \le h(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{p}) \le L_{tether}^2
\end{equation}
where the constraint expression $h(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{p})$ is defined as the squared distance:
\begin{equation}
    h(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{p}) = (x_a - x_{boat})^2 + (y_a - y_{boat})^2 + z_a^2
\end{equation}

In the C++ control pipeline, the current boat coordinates $[x_{boat}, y_{boat}]^T$ are supplied to the solver as time-varying parameters $\mathbf{p}$ along the prediction horizon. To handle situations where external environmental perturbations (such as wind gusts) temporarily pull the quadrotor beyond the nominal tether length, this inequality is implemented as a soft constraint using quadratic slack variables. This prevents optimization solver infeasibility while maintaining a heavy penalty on constraint violations.

\subsection{Virtual Winch Kinematics and Spooling Modes}
\label{subsec:mod_winch_kinematics}

The active winch system on the WAM-V deck dynamically manages the deployed tether length, denoted by $L_{tether}$. The rate of spooling $\dot{L}_{tether}$ is controlled to adapt the tether configuration to the cooperative mission requirements. Two operational modes are formulated for the virtual winch:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geometric Spooling Mode:} The winch tracks the geometric distance $d_{tether}$ between the vehicles. To prevent the cable from becoming overly taut and generating high disturbance forces on the UAV, a positive slack factor $\sigma_{slack}$ is introduced. The target tether length is computed as:
    \begin{equation}
        L_{tether, cmd} = d_{tether} \cdot \left(1 + \sigma_{slack}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\sigma_{slack}$ represents a safety margin allowing a controlled catenary sag.
    \item \textbf{Tension-Regulated Spooling Mode:} The winch adjusts the spooling velocity to maintain a constant target tension $T_{ref}$. The control law for the winch spooling speed $\dot{L}_{tether}$ is defined as a function of the tension tracking error:
    \begin{equation}
        \dot{L}_{tether} = K_{T,p} \left( T_{meas} - T_{ref} \right) + K_{T,d} \dot{T}_{meas}
    \end{equation}
    where $T_{meas}$ is the measured tension at the anchor, and $K_{T,p}$, $K_{T,d}$ are the proportional and derivative gains of the tension controller, respectively.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tether Force and Slack Validation}
\label{subsec:mod_tether_validation}

To evaluate the loading effects on both the \gls{usv} and the \gls{uav} without solving complex partial differential equations online, the physical force exerted by the tether on the vehicles is modeled using a simplified static representation. The tether force vector $\mathbf{F}_{tether}$ acting on the quadrotor is assumed to pull the vehicle directly toward the shipboard anchor point. The magnitude of this force is simplified to the static weight of the deployed (outside) cable segment plus a nominal base tension:
\begin{equation}
    T_0 = T_{base} + \rho_L L_{tether} g
\end{equation}
where $\rho_L$ is the linear mass density of the cable (set to $0.020\,\text{kg/m}$ in the simulation), $L_{tether}$ is the length of the deployed segment outside the winch, and $T_{base}$ is a constant representing the minimum pre-tension.

Assuming the line is straight and taut between the vehicles, the tether force vector $\mathbf{F}_{tether}$ experienced by the UAV is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{tether} = T_0 \frac{\mathbf{p}_{anchor} - \mathbf{p}_a}{\sqrt{\|\mathbf{p}_{anchor} - \mathbf{p}_a\|^2 + \epsilon^2}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_a$ is the UAV position, $\mathbf{p}_{anchor}$ is the global anchor position on the ship deck, and $\epsilon$ is a small positive regularization constant ($10^{-3}$) added to prevent division by zero when the vehicles are coincident.

To validate this simplified force representation, its estimated tension is compared against the high-fidelity dynamic tension solved by the MoorDyn physics engine. Figure~\ref{fig:tether_tension_validation} shows the comparison of the simplified static model against the dynamic tension telemetry from MoorDyn during a coupled simulation run. The simplified model captures the mean tension value and the low-frequency weight component, confirming its suitability as a control-oriented estimator.
