\chapter{System modeling}
\label{chapter:modeling}

Real-time control needs models that are simple enough to compute quickly, yet detailed enough to capture how each vehicle actually moves and interacts with the other. The kinematics, dynamics, and physical coupling of the cooperative system, developed in the sections that follow, let the controller predict how the system evolves and compute feasible, optimal trajectories.\reviewed

Section~\ref{sec:mod_coordinates} fixes the reference coordinate frames and spatial notation used throughout the work. Sections~\ref{sec:mod_usv} and~\ref{sec:mod_uav} then derive the kinematic and dynamic models of the \gls{usv} and the \gls{uav}, including the thruster allocation for the vessel and the tether coupling force linking the two vehicles.

\section{Reference Coordinate Frames and Notation}
\label{sec:mod_coordinates}

Writing the equations of motion for the cooperative system requires a consistent set of coordinate frames and spatial notation. This section defines the global inertial reference frame, the body-fixed frames for each platform, and the unified state notation adopted throughout this work.

\paragraph{Inertial Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$}
This frame establishes the global coordinate system following the East-North-Up (ENU) convention, with origin $\mathcal{O}_I$ on the calm-water surface. The axis $\mathbf{x}_I$ points East, $\mathbf{y}_I$ points North, and $\mathbf{z}_I$ points upward, opposite to gravity. The flat-Earth assumption is adopted because the local cooperative tracking controller operates over a bounded spatial horizon.\reviewed

\paragraph{USV Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$}
This frame is rigidly attached to the surface vessel (USV). Its origin $\mathcal{O}_v$ is located at the vessel's centre of gravity, decoupling linear and rotational momentum equations. The longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_v$ points forward (surge), the lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_v$ points to port (sway, left), and the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_v$ points upward (heave).\reviewed

\paragraph{UAV Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$}
Similarly, this frame attaches to the aerial vehicle (UAV) with origin $\mathcal{O}_a$ at its centre of gravity. The longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_a$ points forward, the lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_a$ points to the left, and the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$ points upward, aligning with the collective thrust direction.\reviewed

\section{WAM-V Surface Vessel Model}
\label{sec:mod_usv}

The mathematical model of the WAM-V catamaran, covering its horizontal maneuvering dynamics and thruster allocation, forms a control-oriented state-space model suitable for trajectory tracking and predictive control.

\subsection{Horizontal Model Reduction and 3-DOF State Definition}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_assumptions}

While the general unconstrained spatial motion of a marine craft comprises six Degrees of Freedom (surge, sway, heave, roll, pitch, and yaw), naval architecture draws a fundamental distinction between \emph{seakeeping} and \emph{manoeuvring} theories \cite{snameNomenclature1950,fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}: seakeeping addresses high-frequency wave-induced oscillatory motions in heave, roll, and pitch, whereas manoeuvring governs the low-frequency horizontal trajectory driven by thrusters and environmental drift.

For trajectory tracking and cooperative control with an aerial vehicle, the USV dynamics are reduced to a 3-DOF horizontal manoeuvring model (\emph{surge, sway, and yaw}) based on the following physical properties:

\paragraph{Planar Motion}
The vehicle operates strictly on the water surface, so out-of-plane velocities are neglected:
\begin{equation}
    w(t) = 0, \quad p(t) = 0, \quad q(t) = 0
\end{equation}
which implies that roll, pitch, and vertical displacement remain negligible ($\phi \approx 0, \; \theta \approx 0, \; z_v \approx 0$).

\paragraph{Hydrostatic Metacentric Restoring}
Positive metacentric stability ($GM_T > 0, \; GM_L > 0$) provides stiff restoring moments in roll, pitch, and heave, ensuring that out-of-plane perturbations are self-stabilising.

\paragraph{Port-Starboard Symmetry}
Symmetry about the longitudinal centerplane ($x_v\text{--}z_v$) causes cross-inertia terms to vanish ($I_{xy} = I_{yz} = 0$), dynamically decoupling horizontal motions from roll, pitch, and heave.

\paragraph{Horizontal Actuation Authority}
The stern thrusters act strictly in the horizontal plane. Because out-of-plane states are not controllable and tether disturbances are small relative to the hull displacement, these degrees of freedom are omitted from the predictive model.

\paragraph{Wave-Frequency Motion Separation}
First-order oscillatory wave forces are treated as high-frequency zero-mean disturbances and are not counteracted by the thrusters to prevent actuator wear, leaving the controller focused purely on low-frequency manoeuvring \cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}.

\paragraph{Reduced 3-DOF State Representation}
Under this planar formulation, the USV state is completely defined by the horizontal position $\mathbf{p}_v = [x_v, y_v]^T \in \mathbb{R}^2$, heading $\psi_v \in [-\pi, \pi)$, and body-fixed velocity $\mathbf{v}_v = [u, v, r]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$.

\subsection{Planar Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_kinematics}

The planar kinematic transformation relating the body linear velocities $[u, v]^T$ to the inertial position rates $\dot{\mathbf{p}}_v$ is given by the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_v(\psi_v) \in SO(2)$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_kinematics_planar}
    \dot{\mathbf{p}}_v = \mathbf{R}_v(\psi_v) \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix} \iff \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_v \\ \dot{y}_v \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi_v & -\sin\psi_v \\ \sin\psi_v & \cos\psi_v \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
with the heading rate kinematic relation:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\psi}_v = r \label{eq:usv_psi_dot}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamics}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_dynamics}

Newton's second law relates the vessel's translational and rotational acceleration to the resultant external force and moment, here produced by the stern thrusters and opposed by hydrodynamic damping. Because the body frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ translates with linear velocity $\mathbf{v} = [u, v, 0]^T$ and rotates with angular rate $\boldsymbol{\omega} = [0, 0, r]^T$, the total inertial acceleration of the center of gravity follows Euler's relation $\mathbf{a} = \dot{\mathbf{v}} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{u} \\ \dot{v} \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ r \end{bmatrix} \times \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{u} - v r \\ \dot{v} + u r \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
For planar translation and rotation about the vertical axis, this yields:
\begin{align}
    m (\dot{u} - v r) &= X - d_u(u) u \label{eq:usv_newton_u} \\
    m (\dot{v} + u r) &= Y - d_v(v) v \label{eq:usv_newton_v} \\
    I_{zz} \dot{r} &= N - d_r(r) r \label{eq:usv_newton_r}
\end{align}
where $m$ and $I_{zz}$ denote the effective mass and yaw moment of inertia (incorporating added mass terms), $X$ and $Y$ are the surge and sway propulsion forces, and $N$ is the yaw control moment.

\paragraph{Hydrodynamic Damping}

The damping functions $d_u(u)$, $d_v(v)$, and $d_r(r)$ combine two physical effects: a term proportional to velocity, from viscous friction along the hull, and a term proportional to the square of velocity, from the pressure drag of the water resisting the hull at higher speed:
\begin{align}
    d_u(u) &= \begin{cases} 
        X_U + X_{UU} |u|, & u \ge 0 \\
        X_U + X_{UU\_BWD} |u|, & u < 0
    \end{cases} \label{eq:damping_u} \\
    d_v(v) &= Y_V + Y_{VV} |v| \label{eq:damping_v} \\
    d_r(r) &= N_R + N_{RR} |r| \label{eq:damping_r}
\end{align}
where $X_U, Y_V, N_R$ are the linear, viscous-friction coefficients and $X_{UU}, X_{UU\_BWD}, Y_{VV}, N_{RR}$ are the quadratic, pressure-drag coefficients. The surge damping distinguishes forward and backward motion due to hull fore-aft asymmetry, whereas lateral hull symmetry produces symmetric sway and yaw damping.

Isolating the accelerations $(\dot{u}, \dot{v}, \dot{r})$ from \eqref{eq:usv_newton_u}--\eqref{eq:usv_newton_r} yields:
\begin{align}
    \dot{u} &= \frac{X - d_u(u) u}{m} + v r \label{eq:usv_surge_dot} \\
    \dot{v} &= \frac{Y - d_v(v) v}{m} - u r \label{eq:usv_sway_dot} \\
    \dot{r} &= \frac{N - d_r(r) r}{I_{zz}} \label{eq:usv_yaw_dot}
\end{align}

\paragraph{Thruster Allocation}

Two steerable stern thrusters $(T_L, \alpha_L)$ and $(T_R, \alpha_R)$ generate the control forces and yaw moment $\boldsymbol{\tau}_v = [X, Y, N]^T$, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:usv_planar_model}:
\begin{align}
    X &= T_L \cos\alpha_L + T_R \cos\alpha_R \label{eq:alloc_X} \\
    Y &= T_L \sin\alpha_L + T_R \sin\alpha_R \label{eq:alloc_Y} \\
    N &= x_{\mathrm{arm}} \left( T_L \sin\alpha_L + T_R \sin\alpha_R \right) - y_{\mathrm{arm}} \left( T_L \cos\alpha_L - T_R \cos\alpha_R \right) \label{eq:alloc_N}
\end{align}
where $T_i$ is the thrust magnitude, $\alpha_i$ is the steering angle of thruster $i \in \{L, R\}$, and $(x_{\mathrm{arm}}, \pm y_{\mathrm{arm}})$ are the thruster mounting locations relative to the center of gravity.

\section{x500 Quadrotor Model}
\label{sec:mod_uav}

The x500 quadrotor model developed here covers its translational flight dynamics together with the inner-loop attitude response.

\subsection{Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_kinematics}

In the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\} = (\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{I}}, \mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{I}}, \mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{I}})$, the quadrotor position $\mathbf{p}_a$ and linear velocity $\dot{\mathbf{p}}_a$ are:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_a = \begin{bmatrix} x_a \\ y_a \\ z_a \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \dot{\mathbf{p}}_a = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_a \\ \dot{y}_a \\ \dot{z}_a \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Roll ($\phi$), pitch ($\theta$), and yaw ($\psi_a$) angles parameterize the orientation of the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\} = (\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{y}_a, \mathbf{z}_a)$. The rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ maps vectors from $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ to $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ using the standard $Z$-$Y$-$X$ intrinsic sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) = \mathbf{R}_z(\psi_a) \, \mathbf{R}_y(\theta) \, \mathbf{R}_x(\phi)
\end{equation}
where the elementary rotation matrices are:
\begin{align}
    \mathbf{R}_z(\psi_a) &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi_a & -\sin\psi_a & 0 \\ \sin\psi_a & \cos\psi_a & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \\
    \mathbf{R}_y(\theta) &= \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 & \sin\theta \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -\sin\theta & 0 & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix} \\
    \mathbf{R}_x(\phi) &= \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\ 0 & \sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{bmatrix}
\end{align}
Multiplying the three rotation matrices together gives:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uav_rot_matrix}
    \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_a \cos\theta & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi - \sin\psi_a \cos\phi & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        \sin\psi_a \cos\theta & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi + \cos\psi_a \cos\phi & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \sin\phi & \cos\theta \cos\phi
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Dynamics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_dynamics}

Newton's second law relates the quadrotor's translational acceleration, expressed in the inertial frame, to the resultant external force, decomposed here into the rotor thrust, gravity, and the tether coupling force.

\paragraph{Thrust Force}

The collective aerodynamic thrust acts along the positive body vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$. With $T_{\mathrm{total}} \ge 0$ denoting the total thrust force and $m_a$ the quadrotor mass, the mass-normalized thrust acceleration is:
\begin{equation}
    a_T = \frac{T_{\mathrm{total}}}{m_a} \quad \left[\text{m/s}^2\right]
\end{equation}
Let $\mathbf{e}_3 = [0, 0, 1]^T$ denote the vertical unit vector, so that the thrust acceleration in the body frame is $\mathbf{a}_{\mathcal{B}} = a_T\, \mathbf{e}_3$. Rotating this vector into the inertial frame gives $\mathbf{a}_{thrust} = \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)\, \mathbf{a}_{\mathcal{B}} = a_T\, \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)\, \mathbf{e}_3$, i.e., $a_T$ times the third column of $\mathbf{R}_a$.

\paragraph{Gravitational Force}

Gravity acts along the vertical direction of the inertial frame, contributing a constant acceleration $\mathbf{g} = [0, 0, -g]^T$ to the quadrotor motion, independent of its attitude.

\paragraph{Tether-Induced Force}
\label{subsec:mod_tether_force_model}

The physical tether attaches to the quadrotor at its center of gravity $\mathbf{p}_a$, generating no attitude moments, and connects to the shipboard winch anchor located at $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} = [x_v, y_v, h_v + h_{\mathrm{anchor}}]^T$. When the line is taut, the tension force pulling the vehicle toward the vessel deck is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{tether} = T_{tether} \frac{\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} - \mathbf{p}_a}{\|\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} - \mathbf{p}_a\|}
\end{equation}
where $T_{tether} \ge 0$ is the tether tension magnitude. During autonomous flight and tracking phases, the active winch maintains a controlled cable catenary ($T_{tether} \approx 0$), mechanically decoupling the quadrotor from wave-induced vessel motions. During tension-guided landing, $T_{tether} > 0$ provides a physical restoring force toward the landing deck.

\paragraph{Resultant Translational Dynamics}

Summing the three forces, the translational acceleration is:
\begin{equation}
    \ddot{\mathbf{p}}_a = a_T\, \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)\, \mathbf{e}_3 + \mathbf{g} + \frac{\mathbf{F}_{tether}}{m_a}
\end{equation}
In nominal unconstrained flight ($T_{tether} \approx 0$), evaluating the acceleration components yields the translational equations of motion, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:uav_thrust_vectoring}:
\begin{align}
    \ddot{x}_a &= a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vx_dot} \\
    \ddot{y}_a &= a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vy_dot} \\
    \ddot{z}_a &= a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi - g \label{eq:uav_vz_dot}
\end{align}

\subsection{Attitude Dynamics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_statespace}

Rather than derive the full rotational dynamics from motor torques, the model exploits the autopilot's inner attitude loop: the autopilot regulates motor speeds to track commanded attitude angles, and a decoupled first-order lag captures the resulting closed-loop response of roll ($\phi$) and pitch ($\theta$) to commanded setpoints ($\phi_{cmd}, \theta_{cmd}$):
\begin{align}
    \dot{\phi} &= \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \phi_{cmd} - \phi \right) \label{eq:uav_roll_dot} \\
    \dot{\theta} &= \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \theta_{cmd} - \theta \right) \label{eq:uav_pitch_dot}
\end{align}
where $\tau > 0$ is the characteristic attitude time constant, identified and validated in Section~\ref{subsec:res_uav_validation}.

Chapter~\ref{chapter:control} augments this physical model with command rate derivatives to guarantee continuous actuation profiles, and integrates the tether geometric boundary into the optimal predictive control framework.
