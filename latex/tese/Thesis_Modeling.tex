\chapter{System modeling}
\label{chapter:modeling}

Real-time control requires simplified yet representative models that describe the motion and physical interaction of each vehicle. To this end, this chapter describes the kinematics, dynamics, and physical coupling of the cooperative system. These formulations allow the controller to predict future states and compute feasible, optimal trajectories for the system.\reviewed

First, the chapter defines the reference coordinate frames and spatial transformations. It then develops the kinematic and dynamic models for the \gls{usv} and the \gls{uav}. Finally, it establishes the geometric and force models for the flexible tether and active winch.\reviewed


\section{Coordinate frames and transformations}
\label{sec:mod_coordinates}

Formulating the equations of motion for the cooperative system requires a consistent set of coordinate frames. To this end, this work defines a common global inertial reference frame alongside individual body-fixed frames for both the USV and the UAV. This formulation simplifies the representation of hydrodynamic and aerodynamic forces relative to each vehicle. Figure~\ref{fig:coordinate_frames} illustrates the spatial relationships between these coordinate systems.\reviewed

\subsection{Reference coordinate frames}

\paragraph{Inertial Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$}
This frame establishes the global coordinate system, following the East-North-Up (ENU) convention. Its origin $\mathcal{O}_I$ lies on the water surface. In this frame, the axis $\mathbf{x}_I$ points East, $\mathbf{y}_I$ points North, and $\mathbf{z}_I$ points upward, opposite to gravity. This flat-Earth assumption is justified because the local tracking controller operates over a short spatial horizon.\reviewed

\paragraph{USV Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$}
This frame attaches directly to the USV. Its origin $\mathcal{O}_v$ lies at the USV's center of gravity, a placement that simplifies the equations of motion by decoupling linear and rotational movements. Regarding the axes, the longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_v$ points forward (surge), the lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_v$ points to port (sway, left), and the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_v$ points upward (heave).\reviewed

\paragraph{UAV Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$}
Similarly, this frame attaches to the UAV. Its origin $\mathcal{O}_a$ resides at the UAV's center of gravity. The longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_a$ points forward, the lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_a$ points to the left, and the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$ points upward, aligning with the collective thrust.\reviewed

\subsection{Coordinate transformations}

To represent the physical locations of both vehicles relative to the global coordinate system, the model defines their position vectors in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$. The vector $\mathbf{p}_v = [x_v, y_v, h_v]^T$ defines the position of the USV's center of gravity, where the model assumes a constant altitude $h_v$ above the water surface to simplify the motion equations by neglecting heave dynamics. Similarly, the vector $\mathbf{p}_a = [x_a, y_a, z_a]^T$ represents the position of the UAV's center of gravity.\reviewed

Projecting coordinates, velocities, and forces from the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ to the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ requires a rotation matrix. Given that the USV operates on the water surface with horizontal propulsion, and its inherent stability minimizes roll and pitch, the model assumes planar motion. Under this assumption, the USV rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_v(\psi)$ performs this projection depending only on the yaw angle $\psi$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_rot_matrix}
    \mathbf{R}_v(\psi) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\
        \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\
        0 & 0 & 1
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}\reviewed

Similarly, projecting the UAV's thrust force from its body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ to the global inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ requires a rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$. Parameterized by the roll ($\phi$), pitch ($\theta$), and yaw ($\psi_a$) angles, this matrix follows the standard ZYX rotation sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uav_rot_matrix}
    \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_a \cos\theta & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi - \sin\psi_a \cos\phi & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        \sin\psi_a \cos\theta & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi + \cos\psi_a \cos\phi & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \sin\phi & \cos\theta \cos\phi
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}\reviewed

In the \gls{usv}, the tether is attached to a winch located at a vertical offset $\mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{anchor}} = [0, 0, h_{\mathrm{anchor}}]^T$ relative to the USV's CG in $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$. The vertical component $h_{\mathrm{anchor}}$ represents the constant height of the anchor point above the CG. Projecting this offset into the global frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ yields the global anchor position:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} = \begin{bmatrix} x_v \\ y_v \\ h_v \end{bmatrix} + \mathbf{r}_{\mathrm{anchor}} = \begin{bmatrix} x_v \\ y_v \\ h_v + h_{\mathrm{anchor}} \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

The tether attachment point on the \gls{uav} is assumed to coincide exactly with its center of gravity. This simplification eliminates any offset in the \gls{uav}'s body frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$, meaning the tether tension force acts directly on the CG and does not introduce attitude coupling moments. Consequently, the global position of the \gls{uav}'s tether connection is equivalent to the \gls{uav}'s position.\reviewed

\section{WAM-V Surface Vessel Model}
\label{sec:mod_usv}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Objective:} Formulate the kinematic and dynamic equations of motion for the WAM-V (Wave Adaptive Modular Vessel) catamaran platform for real-time optimal control.
    \item \textbf{Modeling Hierarchy:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Planar 3-\gls{dof} kinematics on the horizontal sea surface.
        \item Control-oriented non-linear dynamic model based on Fossen's marine craft formulation \cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}.
        \item Actuator dynamics and non-linear thruster allocation mapping (with reverse thrust efficiency loss).
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{USV Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_kinematics}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{State Representation:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Inertial Position Vector:} $\boldsymbol{\eta}_v = [x_v, y_v, \psi]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$, representing planar positions $(x_v, y_v)$ and heading angle $\psi$ in $\{\mathcal{I}\}$.
        \item \textbf{Body-Fixed Velocity Vector:} $\boldsymbol{\nu}_v = [u, v, r]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$, representing surge velocity $u$, sway velocity $v$, and yaw angular rate $r$ in $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Kinematic Equations:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Mappings from body velocities to inertial rate of change:
        \begin{align}
            \dot{x}_v &= u \cos\psi - v \sin\psi \\
            \dot{y}_v &= u \sin\psi + v \cos\psi \\
            \dot{\psi} &= r
        \end{align}
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assumptions \& Physical Rationale:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Motion is assumed strictly planar on the water surface ($z_v = h_v$).
        \item Out-of-plane degrees of freedom (heave $z$, roll $\phi$, pitch $\theta$) are neglected in the control-oriented model due to high catamaran hydrostatic stability and to maintain real-time computational efficiency in the \gls{nmpc} optimizer (while fully preserved in simulation).
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Control-Oriented Dynamic Model (Fossen 3-DOF)}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_control_model}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Model Purpose:} Horizontal 3-\gls{dof} control-oriented dynamic model in $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ according to Fossen's standard marine vessel equations \cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011} for real-time trajectory optimization.
    \item \textbf{Equations of Motion:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Formulated in body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$:
        \begin{align}
            \dot{u} &= \frac{X - d_u(u) u}{m} + v r \label{eq:usv_surge_dot} \\
            \dot{v} &= \frac{Y - d_v(v) v}{m} - u r \label{eq:usv_sway_dot} \\
            \dot{r} &= \frac{N - d_r(r) r}{I_{zz}} \label{eq:usv_yaw_dot}
        \end{align}
        \item \textbf{Inertial Parameters:} $m$ is the vessel mass, and $I_{zz}$ is the moment of inertia about vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_v$.
        \item \textbf{Virtual Control Inputs:} $X$ (surge force), $Y$ (sway force), and $N$ (yaw moment) applied at the center of gravity.
        \item \textbf{Coriolis / Centrifugal Coupling:} $+vr$ and $-ur$ account for acceleration components arising from the rotating reference frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Non-linear Hydrodynamic Damping Model:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Combines linear and quadratic drag terms.
        \item \textbf{Surge Damping with Directional Asymmetry:} Accounts for the geometrical difference between bow and stern:
        \begin{equation}
            d_u(u) = \begin{cases} 
                X_U + X_{UU} |u|, & \text{if } u \ge 0 \quad \text{(forward motion)} \\
                X_U + X_{UU\_BWD} |u|, & \text{if } u < 0 \quad \text{(backward motion)}
            \end{cases}
        \end{equation}
        \item \textbf{Sway Damping:}
        \begin{equation}
            d_v(v) = Y_V + Y_{VV} |v|
        \end{equation}
        \item \textbf{Yaw Damping:}
        \begin{equation}
            d_r(r) = N_R + N_{RR} |r|
        \end{equation}
        \item \textbf{Parameters:} Linear coefficients ($X_U, Y_V, N_R$) and quadratic coefficients ($X_{UU}, X_{UU\_BWD}, Y_{VV}, N_{RR}$).
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Assumptions \& Physical Rationale:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \textbf{Planar Motion:} Motion is assumed strictly planar on the water surface ($z_v = h_v$).
        \item \textbf{Out-of-Plane Dynamics:} Heave ($z$), roll ($\phi$), and pitch ($\theta$) are neglected in the control model due to high catamaran hydrostatic stability, maintaining computational tractability for NMPC (while active in the Gazebo simulation).
        \item \textbf{Added Mass Simplification:} Hydrodynamic added mass effects are absorbed into the effective vessel inertia parameters ($m, I_{zz}$), preserving a diagonal and constant mass matrix for fast real-time NMPC optimization.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Thruster Allocation Mapping}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_propulsion}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Propulsion Configuration:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Dual steerable stern thrusters (azimuth pods).
        \item \textbf{Geometric Layout:} Located at longitudinal moment arm $x_{arm}$ behind \gls{cg}, and lateral moment arms $\pm y_{arm}$ relative to centerline.
        \item \textbf{Actuator Inputs:} Thrust magnitudes $T_L, T_R$ and azimuth angles $\alpha_L, \alpha_R$ for port (left) and starboard (right) thrusters.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Reverse Thrust Efficiency Asymmetry:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Captures propeller efficiency loss during reverse rotation due to blade profile curvature:
        \begin{equation}
            T_{i, eff} = \begin{cases}
                T_i, & \text{if } T_i \ge 0 \\
                0.746 \cdot T_i, & \text{if } T_i < 0
            \end{cases} \quad \text{for } i \in \{L, R\}
        \end{equation}
        where $0.746$ is the reverse thrust degradation multiplier.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Allocation Mapping Equations:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Transformation from physical thruster states $(T_{L, eff}, T_{R, eff}, \alpha_L, \alpha_R)$ to virtual body forces $[X, Y, N]^T$ at CG:
        \begin{align}
            X &= T_{L, eff} \cos\alpha_L + T_{R, eff} \cos\alpha_R \\
            Y &= T_{L, eff} \sin\alpha_L + T_{R, eff} \sin\alpha_R \\
            N &= x_{arm} \left( T_{L, eff} \sin\alpha_L + T_{R, eff} \sin\alpha_R \right) - y_{arm} \left( T_{L, eff} \cos\alpha_L - T_{R, eff} \cos\alpha_R \right)
        \end{align}
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Actuator Bounds \& Constraints:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Physical thrust saturation $T_{\min} \le T_i \le T_{\max}$ and steering limits $|\alpha_i| \le \alpha_{\max}$ define the feasible virtual control bounds $[X_{\min}, X_{\max}] \times [-Y_{\max}, Y_{\max}] \times [-N_{\max}, N_{\max}]$ enforced in the NMPC solver.
    \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Implementation Details:}
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Embedded directly in the low-level C++ control pipeline to map optimal wrench $[X, Y, N]^T$ from the \gls{nmpc} into individual physical thruster setpoints.
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}


\section{x500 Quadrotor Model}
\label{sec:mod_uav}

\subsection{UAV Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_kinematics}

The \gls{uav} operates in 3D airspace. Following standard aircraft modeling conventions \cite{beardSmallUnmannedAircraft2012}, the position vector of the \gls{uav} \gls{cg} in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is denoted by $\mathbf{p}_a = [x_a, y_a, z_a]^T$. The translational velocity vector of the vehicle, also expressed in the inertial reference frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$, is defined as $\mathbf{v}_a = [v_{x,a}, v_{y,a}, v_{z,a}]^T$. The kinematic relationship mapping the rate of change of the global position coordinates to the inertial velocity vector is directly given by:
\begin{align}
    \dot{x}_a &= v_{x,a} \\
    \dot{y}_a &= v_{y,a} \\
    \dot{z}_a &= v_{z,a}
\end{align}

Unlike the \gls{usv}, whose velocity coordinates are defined in the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ to simplify hydrodynamic force mapping, the translational velocities of the quadrotor are formulated in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$. This representation aligns with the design of the control-oriented model and avoids unnecessary coordinate rotations in the translational dynamics. The orientation of the quadrotor body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ relative to the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is defined by the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ given in \eqref{eq:uav_rot_matrix}.


\subsection{Control-Oriented Model (Attitude Lag)}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_control_model}

To achieve a control-oriented model suitable for real-time execution in the \gls{nmpc} framework, the full $6$-DOF rigid-body dynamics of the quadrotor are simplified. Instead of modeling the high-frequency rotational dynamics, rotor speeds, and torque mixing, a cascaded control assumption is adopted. The high-rate attitude inner loop of the PX4 autopilot is assumed to track commanded roll and pitch references with a characteristic first-order response lag.

The actual roll $\phi$ and pitch $\theta$ dynamics are therefore modeled as:
\begin{align}
    \dot{\phi} &= \frac{\phi_{cmd} - \phi}{\tau} \label{eq:uav_roll_dot} \\
    \dot{\theta} &= \frac{\theta_{cmd} - \theta}{\tau} \label{eq:uav_pitch_dot}
\end{align}
where $\tau$ is the inner-loop attitude response time constant, and $\phi_{cmd}$ and $\theta_{cmd}$ represent the commanded roll and pitch angle setpoints, respectively.

To prevent aggressive variations in the commanded setpoints and ensure smooth trajectory planning, the state vector is augmented to include the commanded attitude variables. By defining the time derivatives of these commands as the virtual control inputs, we have:
\begin{align}
    \dot{\phi}_{cmd} &= \dot{\phi}_{cmd, ctrl} \label{eq:uav_roll_cmd_dot} \\
    \dot{\theta}_{cmd} &= \dot{\theta}_{cmd, ctrl} \label{eq:uav_pitch_cmd_dot}
\end{align}
where $\dot{\phi}_{cmd, ctrl}$ and $\dot{\theta}_{cmd, ctrl}$ are the control variables computed by the NMPC. This formulation allows the optimizer to directly penalize the rate of command changes in the cost function, enforcing actuator smoothness and preventing abrupt drone maneuvers that could destabilize the coupled tethered system.

\subsection{Thrust Vectoring and Acceleration Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_acceleration}

The translational motion of the \gls{uav} is driven by the collective thrust generated by the four rotors, which acts along the body-fixed vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$. The thrust force per unit mass (mass-normalized thrust acceleration) is denoted by $a_T$. Using the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ from the body frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ to the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ defined in \eqref{eq:uav_rot_matrix}, the thrust acceleration vector in the global \gls{enu} coordinate system is:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a}_{thrust} = \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ a_T \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
By computing this product using the third column of the rotation matrix, the components of $\mathbf{a}_{thrust} = [a_{x,thrust}, a_{y,thrust}, a_{z,thrust}]^T$ are:
\begin{align}
    a_{x,thrust} &= a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \\
    a_{y,thrust} &= a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \\
    a_{z,thrust} &= a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi
\end{align}
where $\psi_a$ is the yaw heading angle of the drone, which is treated as a slowly varying external parameter in the control horizon.

Combining the thrust acceleration with the gravity vector $\mathbf{g} = [0, 0, -g]^T$, where $g = 9.81\,\text{m/s}^2$ is the acceleration due to gravity, the complete translational dynamics of the quadrotor are formulated in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ as:
\begin{align}
    \dot{v}_{x,a} &= a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vx_dot} \\
    \dot{v}_{y,a} &= a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vy_dot} \\
    \dot{v}_{z,a} &= a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi - g \label{eq:uav_vz_dot}
\end{align}

This formulation couples the attitude states $(\phi, \theta)$ directly to the translational acceleration of the vehicle, allowing the \gls{nmpc} to manipulate the quadrotor's trajectory in the 3D space by adjusting the attitude commands and thrust magnitude.


\section{Tether and Winch Model}
\label{sec:mod_tether_winch}


\subsection{Control-Oriented Geometric Constraints}
\label{subsec:mod_geometric_tether}

For the formulation of the real-time \gls{nmpc} algorithm, modeling the full multi-body or lumped-mass catenary dynamics of the tether inside the optimization prediction horizon is computationally prohibitive. Instead, the coupling effect is modeled as a geometric boundary constraint. The tether is assumed to act as a spherical workspace envelope of radius $L_{tether}$ centered at the USV anchor point $\mathbf{p}_{anchor} = [x_{boat}, y_{boat}, z_{boat}]^T$. 

Assuming the USV moves on the water surface ($z_{boat} = 0$), the geometric distance $d_{tether}$ between the \gls{uav} position $\mathbf{p}_a = [x_a, y_a, z_a]^T$ and the shipboard anchor is expressed as:
\begin{equation}
    d_{tether} = \sqrt{(x_a - x_{boat})^2 + (y_a - y_{boat})^2 + z_a^2}
\end{equation}

To enforce that the UAV remains within the physical boundary of the tether, a non-linear path constraint is formulated in the optimal control problem:
\begin{equation}
    0 \le h(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{p}) \le L_{tether}^2
\end{equation}
where the constraint expression $h(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{p})$ is defined as the squared distance:
\begin{equation}
    h(\mathbf{x}, \mathbf{p}) = (x_a - x_{boat})^2 + (y_a - y_{boat})^2 + z_a^2
\end{equation}

In the C++ control pipeline, the current boat coordinates $[x_{boat}, y_{boat}]^T$ are supplied to the solver as time-varying parameters $\mathbf{p}$ along the prediction horizon. To handle situations where external environmental perturbations (such as wind gusts) temporarily pull the quadrotor beyond the nominal tether length, this inequality is implemented as a soft constraint using quadratic slack variables. This prevents optimization solver infeasibility while maintaining a heavy penalty on constraint violations.

\subsection{Virtual Winch Kinematics and Spooling Modes}
\label{subsec:mod_winch_kinematics}

The active winch system on the WAM-V deck dynamically manages the deployed tether length, denoted by $L_{tether}$. The rate of spooling $\dot{L}_{tether}$ is controlled to adapt the tether configuration to the cooperative mission requirements. Two operational modes are formulated for the virtual winch:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Geometric Spooling Mode:} The winch tracks the geometric distance $d_{tether}$ between the vehicles. To prevent the cable from becoming overly taut and generating high disturbance forces on the UAV, a positive slack factor $\sigma_{slack}$ is introduced. The target tether length is computed as:
    \begin{equation}
        L_{tether, cmd} = d_{tether} \cdot \left(1 + \sigma_{slack}\right)
    \end{equation}
    where $\sigma_{slack}$ represents a safety margin allowing a controlled catenary sag.
    \item \textbf{Tension-Regulated Spooling Mode:} The winch adjusts the spooling velocity to maintain a constant target tension $T_{ref}$. The control law for the winch spooling speed $\dot{L}_{tether}$ is defined as a function of the tension tracking error:
    \begin{equation}
        \dot{L}_{tether} = K_{T,p} \left( T_{meas} - T_{ref} \right) + K_{T,d} \dot{T}_{meas}
    \end{equation}
    where $T_{meas}$ is the measured tension at the anchor, and $K_{T,p}$, $K_{T,d}$ are the proportional and derivative gains of the tension controller, respectively.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Tether Coupling Force Model}
\label{subsec:mod_tether_force_model}

To evaluate the loading effects on both the \gls{usv} and the \gls{uav} without solving complex partial differential equations online, the physical force exerted by the tether on the vehicles is modeled using a simplified static representation. The tether force vector $\mathbf{F}_{tether}$ acting on the quadrotor is assumed to pull the vehicle directly toward the shipboard anchor point. The magnitude of this force is simplified to the static weight of the deployed (outside) cable segment plus a nominal base tension:
\begin{equation}
    T_0 = T_{base} + \rho_L L_{tether} g
\end{equation}
where $\rho_L$ is the linear mass density of the cable (set to $0.020\,\text{kg/m}$ in the simulation), $L_{tether}$ is the length of the deployed segment outside the winch, and $T_{base}$ is a constant representing the minimum pre-tension.

Assuming the line is straight and taut between the vehicles, the tether force vector $\mathbf{F}_{tether}$ experienced by the UAV is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{tether} = T_0 \frac{\mathbf{p}_{anchor} - \mathbf{p}_a}{\sqrt{\|\mathbf{p}_{anchor} - \mathbf{p}_a\|^2 + \epsilon^2}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_a$ is the UAV position, $\mathbf{p}_{anchor}$ is the global anchor position on the ship deck, and $\epsilon$ is a small positive regularization constant ($10^{-3}$) added to prevent division by zero when the vehicles are coincident.
