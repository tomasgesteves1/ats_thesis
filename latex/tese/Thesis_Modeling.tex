\chapter{System modeling}
\label{chapter:modeling}

Real-time control requires simplified yet representative models that describe the motion and physical interaction of each vehicle. To this end, this chapter describes the kinematics, dynamics, and physical coupling of the cooperative system. These formulations allow the controller to predict future states and compute feasible, optimal trajectories for the system.\reviewed

First, the chapter defines the reference coordinate frames and spatial notation. It then develops the kinematic and dynamic control models for the \gls{usv} and the \gls{uav}, including thruster allocation and tether coupling forces.

\section{Reference Coordinate Frames}
\label{sec:mod_coordinates}

Formulating the equations of motion for the cooperative system requires a consistent set of coordinate frames. To this end, this work defines a common global inertial reference frame alongside individual body-fixed frames for both the USV and the UAV. This formulation simplifies the representation of hydrodynamic and aerodynamic forces relative to each vehicle. Figure~\ref{fig:coordinate_frames} illustrates the spatial relationships between these coordinate systems.\reviewed

\paragraph{Inertial Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$}
This frame establishes the global coordinate system, following the East-North-Up (ENU) convention. Its origin $\mathcal{O}_I$ lies on the water surface. In this frame, the axis $\mathbf{x}_I$ points East, $\mathbf{y}_I$ points North, and $\mathbf{z}_I$ points upward, opposite to gravity. This flat-Earth assumption is justified because the local tracking controller operates over a short spatial horizon.\reviewed

\paragraph{USV Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$}
This frame attaches directly to the USV. Its origin $\mathcal{O}_v$ lies at the USV's center of gravity, a placement that simplifies the equations of motion by decoupling linear and rotational movements. Regarding the axes, the longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_v$ points forward (surge), the lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_v$ points to port (sway, left), and the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_v$ points upward (heave).\reviewed

\paragraph{UAV Body-Fixed Reference Frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$}
Similarly, this frame attaches to the UAV. Its origin $\mathcal{O}_a$ resides at the UAV's center of gravity. The longitudinal axis $\mathbf{x}_a$ points forward, the lateral axis $\mathbf{y}_a$ points to the left, and the vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$ points upward, aligning with the collective thrust.\reviewed

\section{WAM-V Surface Vessel Model}
\label{sec:mod_usv}

This section presents the mathematical model of the WAM-V catamaran. The formulation describes the horizontal maneuvering dynamics and thruster allocation, providing a control-oriented state-space model for trajectory tracking and predictive control.

\subsection{Kinematics}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_kinematics}

In marine hydrodynamics \cite{fossenHandbookMarineCraft2011}, a surface vessel exhibits spatial motion described by its generalized coordinates $\boldsymbol{\eta} = [x_v, y_v, z_v, \phi, \theta, \psi]^T$ and body velocities $\boldsymbol{\nu} = [u, v, w, p, q, r]^T$, governed by:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_dynamics_6dof}
    \left( \mathbf{M}_{RB} + \mathbf{M}_A \right) \dot{\boldsymbol{\nu}} + \left( \mathbf{C}_{RB}(\boldsymbol{\nu}) + \mathbf{C}_A(\boldsymbol{\nu}) \right) \boldsymbol{\nu} + \mathbf{D}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\nu} + \mathbf{g}(\boldsymbol{\eta}) = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{prop}} + \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{tether}} + \boldsymbol{\tau}_{\mathrm{env}}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{M}_{RB}$ and $\mathbf{M}_A$ denote the rigid-body and added mass matrices, $\mathbf{C}_{RB}$ and $\mathbf{C}_A$ represent Coriolis and centripetal effects, $\mathbf{D}(\boldsymbol{\nu})\boldsymbol{\nu}$ accounts for hydrodynamic damping, $\mathbf{g}(\boldsymbol{\eta})$ represents hydrostatic restoring forces, and $\boldsymbol{\tau}$ collects propulsion and external loads.

For control design, this general formulation is reduced to horizontal planar motion based on the physical characteristics of the catamaran. First, the vessel operates on the water surface ($z_v \approx 0$). Second, the twin-pontoon geometry provides large hydrostatic restoring moments that passively stabilize roll ($\phi$), pitch ($\theta$), and heave ($w$) near equilibrium. Third, the stern thrusters act solely in the horizontal plane (surge, sway, and yaw). Fourth, tether forces are negligible compared to the large vessel displacement and propulsion thrust.

Under these conditions, out-of-plane motions decouple from the horizontal motion. The planar position and heading in the inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ are parameterized by $\boldsymbol{\eta}_v = [x_v, y_v, \psi]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$, while body velocities in $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ are $\boldsymbol{\nu}_v = [u, v, r]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$, where $u$ is surge, $v$ is sway, and $r$ is yaw rate.

The planar kinematic mapping from body velocities to inertial coordinate rates is given by:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_kinematics_planar}
    \dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_v = \mathbf{R}_v(\psi) \boldsymbol{\nu}_v \iff \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_v \\ \dot{y}_v \\ \dot{\psi} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi & -\sin\psi & 0 \\ \sin\psi & \cos\psi & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ r \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Evaluating the product gives the scalar kinematic equations:
\begin{align}
    \dot{x}_v &= u \cos\psi - v \sin\psi \label{eq:usv_x_dot} \\
    \dot{y}_v &= u \sin\psi + v \cos\psi \label{eq:usv_y_dot} \\
    \dot{\psi} &= r \label{eq:usv_psi_dot}
\end{align}

\subsection{Dynamics and State-Space Model}
\label{subsec:mod_usv_dynamics}

The dynamic equations of motion describe the vessel acceleration resulting from applied forces and moments. Because the body frame $\{\mathcal{B}_v\}$ translates with linear velocity $\mathbf{v} = [u, v, 0]^T$ and rotates with angular rate $\boldsymbol{\omega} = [0, 0, r]^T$, the total inertial acceleration of the center of gravity follows Euler's relation $\mathbf{a} = \dot{\mathbf{v}} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times \mathbf{v}$:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{a} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{u} \\ \dot{v} \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \\ r \end{bmatrix} \times \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ 0 \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{u} - v r \\ \dot{v} + u r \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Applying Newton's second law for planar translation and rotation about the vertical axis yields:
\begin{align}
    m (\dot{u} - v r) &= X - d_u(u) u \label{eq:usv_newton_u} \\
    m (\dot{v} + u r) &= Y - d_v(v) v \label{eq:usv_newton_v} \\
    I_{zz} \dot{r} &= N - d_r(r) r \label{eq:usv_newton_r}
\end{align}
where $m$ and $I_{zz}$ denote the effective mass and yaw moment of inertia (incorporating added mass terms), $X$ and $Y$ are the surge and sway propulsion forces, and $N$ is the yaw control moment.

The damping functions $d_u(u)$, $d_v(v)$, and $d_r(r)$ model combined linear skin friction and quadratic form drag:
\begin{align}
    d_u(u) &= \begin{cases} 
        X_U + X_{UU} |u|, & u \ge 0 \\
        X_U + X_{UU\_BWD} |u|, & u < 0
    \end{cases} \label{eq:damping_u} \\
    d_v(v) &= Y_V + Y_{VV} |v| \label{eq:damping_v} \\
    d_r(r) &= N_R + N_{RR} |r| \label{eq:damping_r}
\end{align}
where $X_U, Y_V, N_R$ are linear coefficients and $X_{UU}, X_{UU\_BWD}, Y_{VV}, N_{RR}$ are quadratic drag coefficients. The surge damping distinguishes forward and backward motion due to hull fore-aft asymmetry, whereas lateral hull symmetry produces symmetric sway and yaw damping.

Isolating the accelerations $(\dot{u}, \dot{v}, \dot{r})$ from \eqref{eq:usv_newton_u}--\eqref{eq:usv_newton_r} yields:
\begin{align}
    \dot{u} &= \frac{X - d_u(u) u}{m} + v r \label{eq:usv_surge_dot} \\
    \dot{v} &= \frac{Y - d_v(v) v}{m} - u r \label{eq:usv_sway_dot} \\
    \dot{r} &= \frac{N - d_r(r) r}{I_{zz}} \label{eq:usv_yaw_dot}
\end{align}
which can be expressed in the compact Fossen matrix form $\mathbf{M}_v \dot{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_v + \mathbf{C}_v(\boldsymbol{\nu}_v)\boldsymbol{\nu}_v + \mathbf{D}_v(\boldsymbol{\nu}_v)\boldsymbol{\nu}_v = \boldsymbol{\tau}_v$:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_dynamics_expanded}
    \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} m & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & m & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & I_{zz} \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{M}_v} \begin{bmatrix} \dot{u} \\ \dot{v} \\ \dot{r} \end{bmatrix}
    + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & -m v \\ 0 & 0 & m u \\ m v & -m u & 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{C}_v(\boldsymbol{\nu}_v)} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ r \end{bmatrix}
    + \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} d_u(u) & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & d_v(v) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & d_r(r) \end{bmatrix}}_{\mathbf{D}_v(\boldsymbol{\nu}_v)} \begin{bmatrix} u \\ v \\ r \end{bmatrix}
    = \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ N \end{bmatrix}}_{\boldsymbol{\tau}_v}
\end{equation}

The control forces and yaw moment $\boldsymbol{\tau}_v = [X, Y, N]^T$ are generated by two steerable stern thrusters $(T_L, \alpha_L)$ and $(T_R, \alpha_R)$, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:usv_planar_model}:
\begin{align}
    X &= T_L \cos\alpha_L + T_R \cos\alpha_R \label{eq:alloc_X} \\
    Y &= T_L \sin\alpha_L + T_R \sin\alpha_R \label{eq:alloc_Y} \\
    N &= x_{\mathrm{arm}} \left( T_L \sin\alpha_L + T_R \sin\alpha_R \right) - y_{\mathrm{arm}} \left( T_L \cos\alpha_L - T_R \cos\alpha_R \right) \label{eq:alloc_N}
\end{align}
where $T_i$ is the thrust magnitude, $\alpha_i$ is the steering angle of thruster $i \in \{L, R\}$, and $(x_{\mathrm{arm}}, \pm y_{\mathrm{arm}})$ are the thruster mounting locations relative to the center of gravity.

Collecting the planar kinematic and dynamic equations, the USV state vector $\mathbf{x}_v \in \mathbb{R}^6$ and control vector $\mathbf{u}_v \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_v = \begin{bmatrix} x_v \\ y_v \\ \psi \\ u \\ v \\ r \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{u}_v = \begin{bmatrix} X \\ Y \\ N \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
The continuous-time non-linear system dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_v = \mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{usv}}(\mathbf{x}_v, \mathbf{u}_v)$ are summarized as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:usv_state_space_full}
    \dot{\mathbf{x}}_v = \begin{bmatrix}
        \dot{\boldsymbol{\eta}}_v \\
        \dot{\boldsymbol{\nu}}_v
    \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
        u \cos\psi - v \sin\psi \\
        u \sin\psi + v \cos\psi \\
        r \\
        \frac{X - d_u(u) u}{m} + v r \\
        \frac{Y - d_v(v) v}{m} - u r \\
        \frac{N - d_r(r) r}{I_{zz}}
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

\section{x500 Quadrotor Model}
\label{sec:mod_uav}

This section presents the mathematical model of the x500 quadrotor. The formulation describes the translational flight dynamics and the inner-loop attitude response, providing a computationally efficient model for trajectory planning and predictive control.

\subsection{Kinematics and Forces}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_kinematics_thrust}

The position $\mathbf{p}_a$ and linear velocity $\mathbf{v}_a$ of the quadrotor are defined in the global inertial frame $\{\mathcal{I}\} = (\mathbf{x}_{\mathcal{I}}, \mathbf{y}_{\mathcal{I}}, \mathbf{z}_{\mathcal{I}})$ following the East-North-Up (ENU) convention:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{p}_a = \begin{bmatrix} x_a \\ y_a \\ z_a \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{v}_a = \begin{bmatrix} v_{x,a} \\ v_{y,a} \\ v_{z,a} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \dot{x}_a \\ \dot{y}_a \\ \dot{z}_a \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
The orientation of the body-fixed frame $\{\mathcal{B}_a\} = (\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{y}_a, \mathbf{z}_a)$ is parameterized by roll ($\phi$), pitch ($\theta$), and yaw ($\psi_a$) angles. Mapping vectors from $\{\mathcal{B}_a\}$ to $\{\mathcal{I}\}$ is performed by the rotation matrix $\mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a)$ using the standard $Z$-$Y$-$X$ intrinsic sequence:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) = \mathbf{R}_z(\psi_a) \, \mathbf{R}_y(\theta) \, \mathbf{R}_x(\phi)
\end{equation}
where the elementary rotation matrices are:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{R}_z(\psi_a) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\psi_a & -\sin\psi_a & 0 \\ \sin\psi_a & \cos\psi_a & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{R}_y(\theta) = \begin{bmatrix} \cos\theta & 0 & \sin\theta \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -\sin\theta & 0 & \cos\theta \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{R}_x(\phi) = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & \cos\phi & -\sin\phi \\ 0 & \sin\phi & \cos\phi \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
Evaluating the matrix product yields:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uav_rot_matrix}
    \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) = \begin{bmatrix}
        \cos\psi_a \cos\theta & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi - \sin\psi_a \cos\phi & \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        \sin\psi_a \cos\theta & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \sin\phi + \cos\psi_a \cos\phi & \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \\
        -\sin\theta & \cos\theta \sin\phi & \cos\theta \cos\phi
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}

The collective aerodynamic thrust acts along the positive body vertical axis $\mathbf{z}_a$. With $T_{\mathrm{total}} \ge 0$ denoting the total thrust force and $m_a$ the quadrotor mass, the mass-normalized thrust acceleration is:
\begin{equation}
    a_T = \frac{T_{\mathrm{total}}}{m_a} \quad \left[\text{m/s}^2\right]
\end{equation}
In the body frame, the thrust acceleration is $\mathbf{a}_{\mathcal{B}} = [0, 0, a_T]^T$, which rotates to $\mathbf{a}_{thrust} = \mathbf{R}_a(\phi, \theta, \psi_a) \mathbf{a}_{\mathcal{B}} = a_T \cdot \mathbf{R}_{a, [:,3]}$ in the inertial frame.

\label{subsec:mod_tether_force_model}
The physical tether attaches to the quadrotor at its center of gravity $\mathbf{p}_a$, generating no attitude moments, and connects to the shipboard winch anchor located at $\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} = [x_v, y_v, h_v + h_{\mathrm{anchor}}]^T$. When the line is taut, the tension force pulling the vehicle toward the vessel deck is modeled as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{F}_{tether} = T_{tether} \frac{\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} - \mathbf{p}_a}{\|\mathbf{p}_{\mathrm{anchor}} - \mathbf{p}_a\|}
\end{equation}
where $T_{tether} \ge 0$ is the tether tension magnitude. During autonomous flight and tracking phases, the active winch maintains a controlled cable catenary ($T_{tether} \approx 0$), mechanically decoupling the quadrotor from wave-induced vessel motions. During tension-guided landing, $T_{tether} > 0$ provides a physical restoring force toward the landing deck.

Applying Newton's second law in the inertial reference frame, the translational acceleration combines thrust, gravity $\mathbf{g} = [0, 0, -g]^T$, and the tether coupling force:
\begin{equation}
    \dot{\mathbf{v}}_a = a_T \cdot \mathbf{R}_{a, [:,3]} + \mathbf{g} + \frac{\mathbf{F}_{tether}}{m_a}
\end{equation}
In nominal unconstrained flight ($T_{tether} \approx 0$), evaluating the acceleration components yields the translational equations of motion, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:uav_thrust_vectoring}:
\begin{align}
    \dot{v}_{x,a} &= a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vx_dot} \\
    \dot{v}_{y,a} &= a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \label{eq:uav_vy_dot} \\
    \dot{v}_{z,a} &= a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi - g \label{eq:uav_vz_dot}
\end{align}

\subsection{Attitude and State-Space Model}
\label{subsec:mod_uav_statespace}

The onboard autopilot regulates motor speeds to track commanded attitude angles. For the vehicle model, the closed-loop tracking response of roll ($\phi$) and pitch ($\theta$) to commanded setpoints ($\phi_{cmd}, \theta_{cmd}$) is characterized by a decoupled first-order lag:
\begin{align}
    \dot{\phi} &= \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \phi_{cmd} - \phi \right) \label{eq:uav_roll_dot} \\
    \dot{\theta} &= \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \theta_{cmd} - \theta \right) \label{eq:uav_pitch_dot}
\end{align}
where $\tau > 0$ is the characteristic attitude time constant, identified and validated in Section~\ref{subsec:res_uav_validation}.

Collecting the translational and rotational differential equations, the physical state vector $\mathbf{x}_a \in \mathbb{R}^8$, control input vector $\mathbf{u}_a \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and parameter vector $\mathbf{p}_a \in \mathbb{R}$ are defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_a = \begin{bmatrix}
        x_a \\ y_a \\ z_a \\ v_{x,a} \\ v_{y,a} \\ v_{z,a} \\ \phi \\ \theta
    \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{u}_a = \begin{bmatrix}
        \phi_{cmd} \\ \theta_{cmd} \\ a_T
    \end{bmatrix}, \quad
    \mathbf{p}_a = \begin{bmatrix}
        \psi_a
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
The continuous-time non-linear system dynamics $\dot{\mathbf{x}}_a = \mathbf{f}_{\mathrm{uav}}(\mathbf{x}_a, \mathbf{u}_a, \mathbf{p}_a)$ are summarized as:
\begin{equation}
    \label{eq:uav_state_space_full}
    \dot{\mathbf{x}}_a = \begin{bmatrix}
        \dot{\mathbf{p}}_a \\
        \dot{\mathbf{v}}_a \\
        \dot{\phi} \\
        \dot{\theta}
    \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix}
        v_{x,a} \\
        v_{y,a} \\
        v_{z,a} \\
        a_T \left( \cos\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi + \sin\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \\
        a_T \left( \sin\psi_a \sin\theta \cos\phi - \cos\psi_a \sin\phi \right) \\
        a_T \cos\theta \cos\phi - g \\
        \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \phi_{cmd} - \phi \right) \\
        \frac{1}{\tau} \left( \theta_{cmd} - \theta \right)
    \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
In Chapter~\ref{chapter:control}, this physical model is augmented with command rate derivatives to guarantee continuous actuation profiles, and the tether geometric boundary is integrated into the optimal predictive control framework.
