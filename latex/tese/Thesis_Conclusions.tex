\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{chapter:conclusions}

This chapter concludes the dissertation, summarising the key achievements of the cooperative \gls{uav}--\gls{usv} control architecture and proposing future directions of research to extend the capabilities of the system.

\section{Summary of Achievements}
\label{sec:concl_summary}

This dissertation successfully designed, implemented, and validated a decentralised cooperative control architecture for a coupled marine-aerial robotic system composed of the \gls{wamv} catamaran and the x500 \gls{uav} physically connected by a flexible tether. The control framework leverages \gls{nmpc} to achieve coordinated path following and target interception while guaranteeing strict safety margins.

The key achievements and contributions of this work are summarised as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Control-Oriented Modelling and Validation:} Developed a simplified, control-oriented model of the coupled system. The \gls{usv} dynamics are captured via a horizontal 3-\gls{dof} Fossen model, calibrated using Gazebo simulator telemetry. The \gls{uav} dynamics incorporate a first-order attitude lag model ($\tau = 0.15\,\text{s}$) representing the closed-loop response of the autopilot, which was successfully identified and validated against PX4 flight telemetry.
    \item \textbf{Decentralised \gls{nmpc} Design:} Formulated individual tracking \gls{nmpc} controllers for both vehicles. The \gls{usv} controller operates at $10\,\text{Hz}$ to calculate virtual force demands, while the \gls{uav} controller runs at $50\,\text{Hz}$ to compute collective thrust and attitude rates. Cooperative coordination is established by supplying the \gls{usv} coordinates as time-varying parameters inside the \gls{uav}'s optimisation horizon, enforcing a soft-constrained tether boundary.
    \item \textbf{High-Performance Implementation:} Implemented the control stack in C++ following a modular \gls{ros}~2 architecture and the strict "Library + ROS Wrapper" design standard. By using CasADi for symbolic differentiation and Acados for solver code-generation, the computational latency was minimised to an average of $1.5\,\text{ms}$ for the \gls{uav} and $0.8\,\text{ms}$ for the \gls{usv}, proving real-time feasibility.
    \item \textbf{Mission-Level Validation:} Evaluated the cooperative system in a high-fidelity co-simulation environment combining Gazebo Harmonic, PX4 \gls{sitl}, and the MoorDyn physics library. The control system successfully executed an integrated search and pursuit mission, patrolling a restricted zone and intercepting a target \gls{usv} teleoperated via a PlayStation controller. The active winch successfully managed the tether tension under irregular manoeuvres.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, this research demonstrates that coupled, tethered operations are a viable and safe method to combine the elevated perspective of \glspl{uav} with the endurance of \glspl{usv}, offering a robust solution for maritime surveillance and security.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:concl_future}

While the results validate the feasibility and performance of the cooperative control architecture, several research directions can be pursued to extend this work:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physical Deployment and Experimental Validation:} The logical next step is the transition from \gls{sitl} simulation to physical deployment. The modular, \gls{ros}-independent design of the logic pipelines ensures that the C++ \gls{nmpc} controllers can be directly compiled and run on the onboard companion computers of the physical \gls{wamv} catamaran and x500 \gls{uav}.
    \item \textbf{Hardware-in-the-Loop (\gls{hil}) Testing:} Before full field trials, \gls{hil} testing should be conducted by running the PX4 flight control firmware on physical autopilot hardware (e.g., Pixhawk) and executing the \gls{ros}~2 control stack on a dedicated companion computer. This will validate the controller under real processor constraints, sensor noise, and communication latency.
    \item \textbf{Disturbance Observer Design:} Integrating estimator algorithms (such as Extended Kalman Filters or disturbance observers) would allow the \gls{nmpc} controllers to estimate external wind and wave forces in real time. Feeding these estimated disturbances back into the parameters of the optimisation solvers would improve trajectory tracking accuracy under severe environmental conditions.
    \item \textbf{Resilient Decentralised Communication:} Investigate the robustness of the decentralised coordinator under communication failures, packet loss, or latency in the \gls{usv}-to-\gls{uav} data link, developing fail-safe behaviours where the vehicles fall back to local safety controllers.
    \item \textbf{Tether Power Delivery and Energy Harvesting:} Explore the physical optimisation of power transfer through the tether to supply the \gls{uav} from the catamaran's main batteries, extending flight times indefinitely. Additionally, energy regeneration strategies could be investigated, utilising the winch motor to harvest energy from the pulling forces exerted by the \gls{uav} during high-tension manoeuvres.
\end{itemize}
