\section{Problem Statement and Objectives}
\label{sec:intro_problem_statement}

% What exactly is the problem?
This work addresses the control of a \gls{usv} and a \gls{uav} connected by a tether, so that they carry out maritime missions autonomously as a single system. Each vehicle must follow its reference within its own limits, while the distance between them stays within the reach of the tether, also under waves and wind, and the \gls{uav} must take off from and land on the moving deck of the \gls{usv}.

% What does the work set out to achieve?
To address this problem, the work pursues four objectives.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item To model both vehicles and the force of the tether on the \gls{uav} accurately enough to predict their motion over the horizon of a controller, and to validate these models.
    \item To design a controller for each vehicle that respects its limits and anticipates the motion of the other vehicle, so that together they keep the separation within the reach of the tether.
    \item To develop the manoeuvres of both vehicles, including the takeoff from and the landing on the moving deck, and missions that chain them into complete operations.
    \item To evaluate the system in trajectory tracking, in the landing on the moving deck under waves and wind, and in complete missions.
\end{enumerate}

% Under which conditions is it studied?
The system is studied in simulation, in a realistic environment that reproduces the dynamics of both vehicles and of the tether, together with the ocean and its disturbances.
